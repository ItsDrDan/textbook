Who this book is for

Introductory Organic and Biological Chemistry for FCC is a free, openly licensed textbook built for CHEM 3B at Fresno City College. It follows the course outline of record for CHEM 3B (effective Spring 2027, C-ID CHEM 102) chapter by chapter, and it was written with students headed into nursing, dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy and the other health professions in mind. You will meet the chemistry first and then see, in every chapter, where that chemistry shows up in a clinic, a pharmacy, a dental operatory or a lab result.

How this book is organized

Each chapter has three kinds of pages.

  • Section pages (for example 4.1–4.10) carry the core reading. Most are remixed from The Basics of General, Organic, and Biological Chemistry by David W. Ball, John W. Hill and Rhonda J. Scott, and from Fundamentals of General, Organic, and Biological Chemistry (LibreTexts). Ten sections that were empty placeholders in the source books were written new for this course (4.10, 7.11, 11.7, 12.9, 13.7–13.10, 14.5 and 15.6).
  • Chapter Guide pages are the instructor's study guide for the chapter: learning objectives mapped to the course outline, Key Ideas, Professor Tips, worked examples in Problem/Solution/Check form, common mistakes, video lessons from the instructor's CHEM 3B playlist, In the Lab notes, and clinical connections for nursing and dental hygiene. Short interactive Try It practice sets are embedded where a skill is introduced.
  • Practice Problems and Practice Games pages close each chapter. The problems are original to this book and include multiple choice, compare-and-contrast, data analysis and clinical-application items, all with worked answers hidden behind an “Answer” toggle. The games pages embed the free study games from Dr. Dan's Chemistry Study Hub that fit the chapter.

How to use it

  1. Read the section pages before lecture; use the Chapter Guide's Key Ideas to check what you should be able to do.
  2. Work the worked examples with the solution covered, then open it and compare.
  3. Do the Try It sets as you go. Progress is saved only in your browser, so nothing is graded or tracked.
  4. Finish with the Practice Problems. If a problem is hard, the matching game on the Practice Games page gives you unlimited fresh versions of the same skill.

Professor Tip

The clinical boxes are not extra reading. The exam questions in a health-science organic course are increasingly written as scenarios, so practice translating a story about a patient into the functional group, property or pathway it is really asking about.

Accessibility

Every figure carries a text description, tables have header rows, headings follow a consistent outline, and hidden answers use a standard disclosure control that works with keyboard and screen readers. The Try It widgets and games are keyboard-operable and include an “Open in a new tab” link for a larger view. If you find a barrier, tell your instructor so it can be fixed in the book rather than worked around.

A note on medical content

The clinical connections explain the chemistry behind things you will see in practice. They are written at the level of a first chemistry course, not as clinical guidance, and they do not give treatment decisions or doses. Your health-program courses and your clinical instructors are the authority on patient care.
