Addictive Alkaloids

Since ancient times, plants have been used for medicinal purposes. One class of substances, called alkaloids, found in many of these plants has been isolated and found to contain cyclic molecules with an amine functional group. These amines are bases. They can react with H3O+ in a dilute acid to form an ammonium salt, and this property is used to extract them from the plant:

\[\ce{R3N + H3O+ + Cl- ⟶[R3NH+]Cl- + H2O} \nonumber \]

The name alkaloid means “like an alkali.” Thus, an alkaloid reacts with acid. The free compound can be recovered after extraction by reaction with a base:

\[\ce{[R3NH+]Cl- + OH- ⟶R3N + H2O + Cl-} \nonumber \]

The structures of many naturally occurring alkaloids have profound physiological and psychotropic effects in humans. Examples of these drugs include nicotine, morphine, and codeine; heroin is made from morphine in the lab. The plant produces these alkaloids, collectively called secondary plant compounds, as chemical defenses against the numerous pests that attempt to feed on the plant (Figure \(\PageIndex{1}\)).

Lecture Connection

In lecture, alkaloids are the payoff for learning amine basicity. Every alkaloid is an amine. So every one can be pulled out of a plant with dilute acid, as a water-soluble ammonium salt, and then freed again with base. Know the four plant sources and the one structural feature they share.

Alkaloids: amines made by plants

Alkaloids are physiologically active nitrogen-containing compounds produced by plants. Nearly all are amines, often with a heterocyclic ring (Section 7.8); they are used as stimulants, anesthetics, and pain relievers, and many are habit forming. Well-known alkaloids include cocaine (coca plant); caffeine (coffee beans and tea leaves; a purine); nicotine (tobacco; a pyridine ring joined to an N-methylpyrrolidine ring); and morphine and codeine (opium poppy). Morphine and codeine each have a 3° amine in a ring, and morphine also has a phenol. Morphine is very addictive, and patients using it long-term need ever-increasing doses for the same effect; codeine, used for milder pain, is addictive too. Eat enough poppy seeds and you can test positive for opiates on a drug screen.

The extraction chemistry in the section text is exactly the Section 7.3 chemistry. Acid converts the free amine, which dissolves in the plant's oils but not in water, into an ammonium salt that dissolves in water. Base then takes the H+ back off and frees the amine. The same pair of reactions explains the two forms of cocaine the text describes: the hydrochloride salt dissolves in mucous membranes, while the free base is volatile enough to be smoked.

Clinical Connection: Nursing

Opioids such as morphine, codeine, and the semisynthetic hydrocodone and oxycodone are alkaloids: each has a 3° amine in a ring, and morphine also has a phenol OH. On the unit they arrive as salts, morphine sulfate or hydromorphone hydrochloride, because the ammonium salt dissolves for injection and oral absorption while the free base does not. Naloxone, the reversal agent, shares the same amine-containing skeleton with a different group on the nitrogen, which is why it fits the same receptor.

Fentanyl and naloxone. Fentanyl is a fully synthetic opioid: it is made in a lab, not taken from the poppy, so strictly it is not an alkaloid. You can still classify both of its nitrogens. The N in its six-membered ring is a tertiary (3°) amine, the basic group that lets it be given as a salt (fentanyl citrate). The other N is a tertiary amide: it is bonded to the C=O carbon, a benzene ring and a ring carbon, so it carries no H. In California, fentanyl was involved in 7,137 overdose deaths in 2023.1 Naloxone (Narcan) rapidly reverses an opioid overdose, and on March 29, 2023, the FDA approved the 4 mg nasal spray for sale without a prescription,2 so nurses and dental hygienists may be the ones who show patients and families how to use it.

Sources: 1. California Department of Public Health, Opioid-Related Overdose Deaths in California, 2023, 2025. 2. U.S. Food and Drug Administration, FDA Approves First Over-the-Counter Naloxone Nasal Spray, 2023.

Clinical Connection: Dental Hygiene

Nicotine, the double heterocyclic amine drawn in Section 7.8, is the alkaloid in tobacco-cessation lozenges, gum, and patches. In the lozenge, nicotine is present as a salt (nicotine polacrilex, bound to an acidic resin). The lozenge also contains a carbonate buffer that makes the saliva around it slightly basic. That releases the free amine, which is absorbed through the lining of the mouth. It is the same salt-to-free-base shift you saw for local anesthetics in Section 7.3.

Summary

  • Alkaloids are nitrogen-containing bases made by plants, often heterocyclic amines with strong effects on the body (caffeine, nicotine, morphine, quinine, cocaine).
  • Because they are amines, alkaloids dissolve in acid as ammonium salts and are recovered as the free base when a base is added.
  • Many alkaloid drugs are given as salts (for example morphine sulfate) because the salts dissolve in water.
