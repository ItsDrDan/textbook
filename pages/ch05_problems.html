Problem 5.52

Which is the correct IUPAC name for CH3CH(OH)CH2COCH3?

  1. 2-hydroxy-4-pentanone
  2. 4-hydroxy-2-pentanone
  3. 4-oxo-2-pentanol
  4. 2,4-pentanediol
Answer

Problem 5.53

Heating CH3CH(OCH2CH3)2 with water and a trace of acid gives

  1. ethanal and one molecule of ethanol
  2. ethanal and two molecules of ethanol
  3. ethanoic acid and two molecules of ethanol
  4. ethanol only
Answer
B. The compound is an acetal (one carbon with two OCH2CH3 groups). Hydrolysis reverses both formation steps: each OR group leaves as ethanol, and the acetal carbon, which still holds an H and a CH3, becomes the aldehyde ethanal. No oxidation occurs, so no acid forms.

Problem 5.54

Which compound has no chiral carbon?

  1. 2-chlorobutane
  2. 3-methylhexane
  3. 2-methylpropanal
  4. 2-bromopentane
Answer
C. In 2-methylpropanal, (CH3)2CHCHO, C2 carries two identical CH3 groups. In A, B, and D the marked carbon carries four different groups (for example, C2 of 2-chlorobutane: H, Cl, CH3, CH2CH3).

Problem 5.55

An unknown gives a silver mirror with Tollens’ reagent, a brick-red precipitate with Benedict’s reagent, and no yellow solid in the iodoform test. Which could it be?

  1. butanone
  2. butanal
  3. ethanal
  4. 3-pentanone
Answer
B. The two positive oxidation tests require an aldehyde, which rules out both ketones (A and D). Ethanal, CH3CHO, has a CH3 next to its C=O and gives a positive iodoform test, so it is ruled out by the negative result. Butanal has no CH3–C=O unit and fits all three observations.

Problem 5.56

Compare and contrast 2-pentanone and 2-pentanol. (a) Which boils higher, and why? (b) Which is more soluble in water? (c) Give the product, if any, when each is treated with an oxidizing agent, and when each is reduced with H2/Ni. (d) Which is chiral?

Answer
(a) 2-Pentanol (about 119 °C) boils higher than 2-pentanone (about 102 °C). Both have a polar C–O, but only the alcohol has an O–H and can hydrogen bond with itself. (b) Both have five carbons and one oxygen, so both are only moderately soluble; the alcohol is somewhat more soluble because its OH both donates and accepts hydrogen bonds with water, whereas the ketone O only accepts. (c) Oxidation: 2-pentanol (a 2° alcohol) → 2-pentanone; 2-pentanone (a ketone) → no reaction. Reduction: 2-pentanone + H2 → 2-pentanol; 2-pentanol is already reduced and does not add H2. The two compounds are an oxidation–reduction pair. (d) Only 2-pentanol: its C2 holds H, OH, CH3, and CH2CH2CH3. The C2 of 2-pentanone is a C=O carbon with only three neighbors.

Problem 5.57

Predict what happens when each compound is dissolved in water with a trace of acid: (a) 4-hydroxybutanal, HOCH2CH2CH2CHO; (b) hydroxyethanal, HOCH2CHO. For any ring that forms, give the number of atoms in the ring and classify the product.

Answer
(a) The OH on C4 adds to the C=O on C1, closing a five-membered ring (C1, C2, C3, C4, and the oxygen). The product is a cyclic hemiacetal: the old C1 now carries an OH and the ring oxygen. (b) Hydroxyethanal cannot form a ring: the OH is on the carbon next to the carbonyl, and a three-membered ring is far too strained. It stays as the open-chain hydroxy aldehyde (it could react with a separate alcohol molecule to form an ordinary hemiacetal). Rings of five or six atoms form readily; that is why glucose (OH on C5) and fructose (OH on C5 or C6) close so easily in Chapter 8.

Problem 5.58

The table lists four compounds of similar molar mass with their boiling points, but the names have been removed. The compounds are hexane, pentanal, 1-pentanol, and 2-pentanone. Match each letter to a compound and explain your reasoning.

W8669negative
X86103positive
Y86102negative

Answer
W is hexane: lowest boiling point because it has only dispersion forces. Z is 1-pentanol: highest boiling point because of hydrogen bonding (and the only one with molar mass 88). Problem 5.59

Use the Fischer projection shown. (a) How many chiral carbons does the molecule have? (b) What is the maximum number of stereoisomers for this structure? (c) Is the compound D or L? (d) Describe the Fischer projection of its enantiomer.

Answer
(a) Two: C2 (H, OH, CHO, and the rest of the chain) and C3 (H, OH, CH2OH, and the rest of the chain). C1 is a C=O carbon and C4 is a CH2, so neither is chiral. (b) 22 = 4 stereoisomers (two pairs of enantiomers). (c) Look at the chiral carbon farthest from the CHO, which is C3. Its OH points to the right, so the compound is D. More Practice: Clinical and Integrative

Problem 5.60

A nurse screens an infant’s urine two ways. A glucose-oxidase dipstick (enzyme-based, glucose-specific) reads negative, but a copper-reduction tablet test (Clinitest, a Benedict’s-type reagent) turns orange-red. (a) What functional-group feature must the substance in the urine have to give the copper test? (b) Why do the two tests disagree? (c) Name a sugar other than glucose that could explain the result, and the condition the result might point to. (Chemistry context only; follow-up is a clinical decision.)

Answer
(a) A Benedict’s-type test is positive for an aldehyde group (or an α-hydroxy ketone that the basic reagent converts to one). Cu2+ (blue) is reduced to Cu2O (orange-red) while the aldehyde is oxidized to a carboxylate. (b) The copper reagent reacts with any reducing sugar, while glucose oxidase is an enzyme that fits only glucose. A positive copper test with a negative enzyme test means a reducing sugar other than glucose is present. (c) Galactose (an aldehyde sugar) or lactose (a disaccharide that still has a free hemiacetal) would react. In a newborn, galactose in the urine is one sign of galactosemia, an inherited inability to metabolize galactose (Chapter 8 and Chapter 13).

Problem 5.61

A dental office soaks heat-sensitive instruments in a 2% glutaraldehyde solution, OHC–CH2CH2CH2–CHO. (a) Give the IUPAC name of glutaraldehyde. (b) Would it give a positive Tollens’ test? (c) Each aldehyde group can react with an amino group (–NH2) on a protein. Explain, in one or two sentences, why a molecule with two aldehyde groups is a more effective sterilant than one with a single aldehyde group. (d) Why must instruments be rinsed thoroughly after soaking?

Answer
(a) Pentanedial: a five-carbon chain with an aldehyde at each end (the suffix -dial means two -al groups; no numbers are needed because the aldehyde carbons must be C1 and C5). (b) Yes. Both aldehyde groups can be oxidized to carboxylic acids, depositing a silver mirror. (c) One aldehyde can attach to one protein site, but two aldehydes on the same molecule can link two protein sites together, cross-linking the microbe’s proteins and enzymes into a rigid, nonfunctional network; this is the same chemistry formalin uses to fix tissue. (d) The same reactivity that cross-links microbial proteins also irritates skin, eyes, and mucous membranes of the patient and staff, so residual glutaraldehyde must be removed.

Problem 5.62

In diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), the liver releases three "ketone bodies": acetone, CH3COCH3; acetoacetic acid, CH3COCH2COOH; and β-hydroxybutyric acid, CH3CH(OH)CH2COOH. (a) Identify every functional group in each compound. (b) Which one is responsible for the fruity "acetone breath", and what physical property explains why it appears in the breath? (c) Which of the three is a true ketone, and which is chiral? (d) Which two make the blood acidic?

Answer
(a) Acetone: one ketone. Acetoacetic acid: a ketone and a carboxylic acid. β-Hydroxybutyric acid: an alcohol (2°) and a carboxylic acid; it has no C=O other than the acid, so despite the name it is not a ketone. (b) Acetone. It is a small, neutral molecule with no O–H, so it is volatile (boiling point 56 °C) and evaporates from the blood into the air in the lungs. (c) Acetone and acetoacetic acid are ketones. Only β-hydroxybutyric acid is chiral: C3 carries H, OH, CH3, and CH2COOH. (d) Acetoacetic acid and β-hydroxybutyric acid, the two carboxylic acids (Chapter 6); their buildup lowers blood pH. Acetone is neutral. Ketone body formation is covered in Chapter 15.

Problem 5.63

Integrative (Chapters 4 and 5). In the liver, ethanol is converted in two steps: ethanol → compound A → acetic acid. (a) Name compound A and classify both steps as oxidation or reduction. (b) Which of the three compounds would give a positive Tollens’ test? (c) Compound A is also what you would get by oxidizing which class of alcohol? Could a 3° alcohol be converted the same way? (d) Compound A reacts with a molecule of methanol to form a hemiacetal; draw (write) the product.

Answer
(a) Compound A is ethanal (acetaldehyde), CH3CHO. Both steps are oxidations: the 1° alcohol loses two H to become the aldehyde, then the aldehyde gains an O to become the carboxylic acid (the enzymes are alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase). (b) Only ethanal, the aldehyde. Ethanol has no C=O, and acetic acid is already fully oxidized. (c) A 1° alcohol; aldehydes come only from 1° alcohols. A 3° alcohol has no H on the carbon that bears the OH, so it cannot be oxidized to a carbonyl at all. (d) CH3CH(OH)OCH3, 1-methoxyethanol: the carbonyl carbon now holds one OH and one OCH3.

Problem 5.64

Identify each compound as an aldehyde, a ketone, an alcohol, or an ether, and name the functional group's position: (a) CH3CH2CH2CH2CH2CHO; (b) CH3CH2COCH2CH2CH3; (c) CH3OCH2CH2CH3; (d) (CH3)2CHCHO; (e) CH3CH2CH2CH(OH)CH3.

Answer
(a) Aldehyde: the chain ends in CHO (hexanal). (b) Ketone: the CO between two carbons must be C=O, because that carbon has only one bond left for oxygen and oxygen needs two (3-hexanone). (c) Ether: an O between two carbons with no H (methyl propyl ether, 1-methoxypropane). (d) Aldehyde: (CH3)2CH– is just a branched alkyl group attached to a CHO (2-methylpropanal). (e) Alcohol, 2° (2-pentanol). Only (a) and (d) would react with Tollens' reagent.

Problem 5.65

Write the organic product formed when each compound is mixed with Tollens' reagent. If there is no reaction, write "no reaction." (a) hexanal; (b) 3-hexanone; (c) 2-methylbutanal; (d) cyclohexanone.

Answer
(a) Hexanoic acid, CH3(CH2)4COOH (as its carboxylate in the basic reagent), with a silver mirror. (b) No reaction: a ketone has no H on the carbonyl carbon. (c) 2-Methylbutanoic acid, CH3CH2CH(CH3)COOH; the branch does not matter, the CHO is what reacts. (d) No reaction: cyclohexanone is a ketone.

Problem 5.66

Predict the result (red precipitate or no reaction) when each compound is warmed with Benedict's reagent, and explain: (a) butanal; (b) 2-butanone; (c) 1-hydroxy-2-propanone, HOCH2COCH3; (d) 2-propanol.

(a) Red precipitate of Cu2O: aldehydes are oxidized by Cu2+. (b) No reaction: a simple ketone. (c) Red precipitate: the ketone has an OH on the carbon next to the C=O (an α-hydroxy ketone), which Benedict's reagent oxidizes; this is the case that makes fructose a reducing sugar in Chapter 8. (d) No reaction: an alcohol has no carbonyl group, and Benedict's reagent is not strong enough to oxidize it.

Problem 5.67

Give the product of reaction with [H] (a reducing agent such as H2/Pd or NaBH4), and classify any alcohol formed. If there is no reaction, say so. (a) 3-methylbutanal; (b) 3-pentanone; (c) 1-butanol; (d) benzaldehyde.

Problem 5.68

Describe the intermolecular forces present (a) between two molecules of butanal, (b) between a molecule of butanone and a molecule of water, and (c) between two molecules of 1-butanol. Then use your answers to explain why butanone (80 °C) boils well below 1-butanol (118 °C) but is still quite soluble in water.

Answer
(a) Dispersion forces and dipole–dipole forces from the polar C=O. No hydrogen bonding: neither molecule has an H bonded to O. (b) Dispersion, dipole–dipole, and hydrogen bonding: the carbonyl oxygen accepts a hydrogen bond from water's O–H. (c) Dispersion, dipole–dipole, and hydrogen bonding between the O–H groups. Boiling point depends on the forces between molecules of the same compound, so the alcohol, with hydrogen bonding, boils higher. Solubility depends on the forces with water, and the ketone's oxygen can accept hydrogen bonds from water, so a four-carbon ketone dissolves readily.

Problem 5.69

The two Fischer projections below show 2,3-dihydroxybutanal. Are P and Q enantiomers, diastereomers, or the same molecule? Explain, then give the D/L label of each.

Answer
Enantiomers. Compare carbon by carbon: at C2, P has OH on the right and Q has OH on the left; at C3, P has OH on the left and Q has OH on the right. Every horizontal group is swapped, so Q is the exact mirror image of P, and two Fischer projections that are complete mirror images cannot be rotated into each other. D/L is read at the chiral carbon farthest from the CHO (C3): P has that OH on the left, so P is L; Q has it on the right, so Q is D. Enantiomers always carry opposite D/L labels.

Problem 5.70

The three Fischer projections below show 2-chloro-3-hydroxybutanal. (a) Classify each pair, X/Y, X/Z, and Y/Z, as enantiomers, diastereomers, or identical. (b) Which of the three is an L compound? (c) How many stereoisomers does this compound have in total?

Answer
(a) X/Y: diastereomers. They differ only at C2 (Cl right vs. left); C3 is the same, so they are stereoisomers but not mirror images. X/Z: enantiomers. Both C2 and C3 are swapped, so Z is the complete mirror image of X. Y/Z: diastereomers. They match at C2 (Cl on the left) and differ at C3. (b) Read C3, the chiral carbon farthest from the CHO: X and Y have the OH on the right (D); Z has it on the left, so Z is L. (c) Two chiral carbons, so 22 = 4 stereoisomers; the fourth (not drawn) is the enantiomer of Y, with Cl on the right at C2 and OH on the left at C3.

Problem 5.71

Which molecule contains an acetal carbon (two –OR groups on the same carbon)?

  1. CH3OCH2CH2OCH3
  2. CH3CH2CH(OH)OCH3
  3. CH3CH(OCH2CH3)2
  4. CH3COOCH3
Answer
C. Its C2 carries two OCH2CH3 groups: an acetal made from ethanal and two ethanols. In A the two ether oxygens are on different carbons, so it is a simple diether. B has one OH and one OR on the same carbon, which makes it a hemiacetal, not an acetal. D is an ester (the carbon with two oxygens is a C=O carbon, Chapter 6).

Problem 5.72

Fill in the blanks. When 2-pentanone reacts with one molecule of methanol, the product is a ________ in which one carbon holds an ________ group and an ________ group. Reaction with a second methanol gives a(n) ________ plus ________. Because the starting carbonyl compound was a ketone, these products may also be called a ________ and a ________.

Answer
The product is a hemiacetal in which one carbon holds an OH group and an OCH3 (OR) group. Reaction with a second methanol gives an acetal plus water. Because the starting compound was a ketone, these may also be called a hemiketal and a ketal. Structures: CH3C(OH)(OCH3)CH2CH2CH3, then CH3C(OCH3)2CH2CH2CH3.
