Learning Objectives

  • Name carboxylic acids, carboxylate salts, and esters.
  • Explain the high boiling points of carboxylic acids and compare them with esters, alcohols, and aldehydes.
  • Write the ionization of a carboxylic acid in water and its neutralization with a strong base.
  • Draw the ester formed from an acid and an alcohol, and the products of acid and base hydrolysis (saponification).

Key Ideas

1. Structures and names

  • Carboxylic acid: R–COOH. IUPAC: replace -e with -oic acid, with the COOH carbon as C1: butanoic acid. Common names to know: formic (1 C), acetic (2 C), propionic (3 C), butyric (4 C), benzoic acid.
  • Carboxylate ion/salt: R–COO−. Change -ic acid to -ate: sodium ethanoate (sodium acetate).
  • Ester: R–COO–R′. Name the alcohol part first as an alkyl group, then the acid part with an -ate ending: methyl ethanoate (methyl acetate).

Professor Tip

In an ester, the side with the C=O came from the carboxylic acid. The side attached through the single-bonded O came from the alcohol. Find the C=O, split the molecule at the single-bonded O, and you can name it or "un-make" it in seconds.

2. Properties

  • Carboxylic acids hydrogen bond in pairs (dimers), giving them the highest boiling points of the groups we have studied so far. They are also very soluble in water when small.
  • Esters have no O–H, so they cannot hydrogen bond with each other. Their boiling points are lower than alcohols of similar size. Many small esters have pleasant fruity smells.

Boiling-point trend for similar molar masses: alkane < ester ≈ aldehyde/ketone < alcohol < carboxylic acid.

3. Reactions

  • Weak acid in water: RCOOH + H2O ⇌ RCOO− + H3O+ (only slightly ionized).
  • Neutralization: RCOOH + NaOH → RCOO−Na+ + H2O. Carboxylate salts are ionic and much more soluble in water than the acid.
  • Esterification (a condensation reaction): RCOOH + R′OH (H+, heat) ⇌ RCOOR′ + H2O
  • Acid hydrolysis (the reverse): ester + H2O (H+) → carboxylic acid + alcohol
  • Base hydrolysis (saponification): ester + NaOH → carboxylate salt + alcohol. This is how soap is made from fats (Chapter 9).

4. Substituents and benzoic acid derivatives

  • Because carboxylic acids have the highest naming priority, other groups appear as prefixes: CH3COCH2COOH is 3-oxobutanoic acid (acetoacetic acid, a ketone body you will meet in Chapter 15). CH3CH(OH)COOH is 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid).
  • Benzoic acid derivatives are numbered from the COOH carbon (C1): 2-hydroxybenzoic acid is salicylic acid (the parent of aspirin), and 4-aminobenzoic acid is PABA (released when benzocaine is hydrolyzed).
  • The acid-catalyzed reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol is called Fischer esterification.

Health Career Connection

  • Dental hygiene – topical anesthetics: Benzocaine, the active ingredient in topical numbing gels used before injections and in oral pain gels, is an ester. Ester-type anesthetics are hydrolyzed quickly by esterase enzymes in the blood. One of the hydrolysis products (PABA) is behind most true allergies to this class. That is why patients are asked about anesthetic allergies, and why amide-type anesthetics (Chapter 7) are used for injections.
  • Nursing – aspirin: Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) contains both a carboxylic acid and an ester. Old aspirin tablets that smell like vinegar have been hydrolyzed by moisture, releasing acetic acid. They should be discarded.
  • Nursing – hand hygiene: Soap is a carboxylate salt. Its long nonpolar tail dissolves grease and its ionic head dissolves in water, which lets rinse water carry oils and microbes away.
  • Physiology: Lactic acid from muscle and oral bacteria is a carboxylic acid. At body pH it exists mostly as lactate, the carboxylate ion.
  • Dental hygiene – acid erosion and the critical pH: Enamel is the mineral hydroxylapatite, Ca5(PO4)3OH. Below a mouth pH of about 5.5 it starts to dissolve: Ca5(PO4)3OH + 4 H+ → 5 Ca2+ + 3 HPO42− + H2O. Lactic acid from plaque bacteria causes caries. Carboxylic acids in food and drinks, such as citric acid in juices and sports drinks, cause erosion even without bacteria. Patients with erosion are often advised to wait about an hour after acidic food before brushing, because softened enamel is easily brushed away.

Further reading: M. Epple and J. Enax, "The Chemistry of Dental Care," ChemistryViews (Part 1, Part 2).

Worked Examples

Example 1: Naming an acid and its salt

Name CH3CH(CH3)CH2COOH and its sodium salt.

Solution

The COOH carbon is C1. The four-carbon chain has a methyl on C3: 3-methylbutanoic acid. The sodium salt is sodium 3-methylbutanoate.

Example 2: Making and naming an ester

Acetic acid reacts with 1-pentanol (H+ catalyst). Draw and name the ester. (It smells like pears.)

Solution

CH3COOH + HOCH2CH2CH2CH2CH3 → CH3COOCH2CH2CH2CH2CH3 + H2O

Alcohol part first, then the acid part: pentyl ethanoate (pentyl acetate). You will make this ester in Experiment 6.

Example 3: Saponification

Give the products when ethyl butanoate, CH3CH2CH2COOCH2CH3, is heated with NaOH.

Solution

Split at the single-bonded O. The C=O side becomes the carboxylate salt and the other side becomes the alcohol:

CH3CH2CH2COO−Na+ (sodium butanoate) + CH3CH2OH (ethanol)

Example 4: Boiling point ranking

Acetic acid, 1-propanol, and methyl formate all have a molar mass of 60 g/mol. Rank their boiling points.

Solution

Methyl formate (ester, no self hydrogen bonding, 32 °C) < 1-propanol (hydrogen bonding, 97 °C) < acetic acid (hydrogen-bonded dimers, 118 °C).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Naming esters in the wrong order. The alkyl group from the alcohol comes first.
  • Forgetting the water. Esterification releases H2O, and hydrolysis uses it.
  • Writing a carboxylic acid (instead of its salt) as the product of base hydrolysis.
  • Calling a carboxylic acid a strong acid. It is a weak acid and only partly ionizes.

Exercise 1

Name CH3CH2COOCH3.

Answer
Methyl propanoate

Exercise 2

Write the neutralization of benzoic acid with KOH. Why is the product used as a food preservative instead of the acid?

Answer
C6H5COOH + KOH → C6H5COO−K+ + H2O. The salt (potassium benzoate) is much more water soluble, so it dissolves easily in drinks and foods.

Exercise 3

What acid and alcohol would you combine to make ethyl methanoate (rum flavor)?

Answer
Methanoic acid (formic acid, HCOOH) and ethanol (CH3CH2OH)

Exercise 4

Give the products of the acid hydrolysis of methyl benzoate.

Answer
Benzoic acid + methanol

Exercise 5

Name CH3CHClCOOH.

Answer
2-chloropropanoic acid
