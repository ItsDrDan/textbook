Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 8 of 15

Sugar, fiber and xylitol gum.

At her next nutrition visit, Rosa learns to count carbohydrates. The dietitian points out that starch in tortillas and rice is digested to glucose, while the fiber in beans and vegetables, including cellulose, is not digested; it passes through without being absorbed, and soluble fiber slows the rise in blood glucose after a meal. Table sugar, sucrose, breaks down into glucose and fructose.

For her dry mouth, the hygienist suggests chewing sugar-free gum sweetened with xylitol. Chewing increases saliva, and the mouth bacteria that make acid cannot ferment xylitol.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Registered Dietitian

Dietitians teach people with diabetes to count carbohydrates and read food labels. Telling a sugar from a starch from a fiber, and knowing which ones raise blood glucose, is this chapter’s chemistry.
