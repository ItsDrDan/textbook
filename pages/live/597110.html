Learning Objectives

  • To describe the preparation of carboxylic acids.

As we noted previously, the oxidation of aldehydes or primary alcohols forms carboxylic acids:

In the presence of an oxidizing agent, ethanol is oxidized to acetaldehyde, which is then oxidized to acetic acid.

This process also occurs in the liver, where enzymes catalyze the oxidation of ethanol to acetic acid.

\[\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH \underset{oxidizing\: agent}{\xrightarrow{alcohol\: dehydrogenase}} CH_3CHO \underset{oxidizing\: agent}{\xrightarrow{aldehyde\: dehydrogenase}} CH_3COOH} \nonumber \]

Acetic acid can be further oxidized to carbon dioxide and water.

Summary

Whether in the laboratory or in the body, the oxidation of aldehydes or primary alcohols forms carboxylic acids.

Lecture Connection

This short section closes the oxidation ladder you started in Chapter 4: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid with [O]. In lecture the ladder is the single best way to keep the alcohol, aldehyde and acid families connected.

The oxidation ladder

  • Making an acid: oxidation of a 1° alcohol. 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid with [O] (Chapter 4; you made these products in the alcohol oxidation lab). The section's example is the one your liver runs: ethanol → acetaldehyde → acetic acid, with alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase doing the oxidizing.
  • Reduction runs part of the ladder backward with [H]: an aldehyde is reduced to a 1° alcohol and a ketone to a 2° alcohol (Chapter 5). Reduction of carboxylic acids is not part of this course. A 2° alcohol never oxidizes past the ketone.
  • A 3° alcohol sits off the ladder entirely: no H on its C–OH carbon, so no oxidation and no carbonyl to come from.

Example: The oxidation ladder

Problem. (a) What aldehyde and what carboxylic acid form when 3-methyl-1-butanol is oxidized with excess [O]? (b) Which alcohol would you oxidize to make 3-methylbutanoic acid? (c) Could a 2° or 3° alcohol be oxidized to a carboxylic acid?

Solution. (a) 3-Methyl-1-butanol, (CH3)2CHCH2CH2OH, is a 1° alcohol. It is oxidized first to the aldehyde 3-methylbutanal, (CH3)2CHCH2CHO, and then to the acid 3-methylbutanoic acid, (CH3)2CHCH2COOH. (b) The same 1° alcohol, 3-methyl-1-butanol: keep every carbon and turn the COOH back into CH2OH. (c) No. A 2° alcohol is oxidized only to a ketone, and a 3° alcohol is not oxidized at all. Only 1° alcohols (through the aldehyde) give carboxylic acids.

Check. Write the ladder: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid, with [O] at each step. The carbon count (five) is the same on every rung.

Professor Tip

Every rung of the ladder keeps the same carbon skeleton; only the oxidation state of one carbon changes. If a question gives you an acid and asks what alcohol it came from, keep every carbon, turn the COOH into CH2OH, and you have a 1° alcohol every time.
