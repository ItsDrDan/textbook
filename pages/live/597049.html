Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 11 of 15

How Rosa’s medicines work.

Rosa makes a chart of her medicines for a class at the senior center where she volunteers. Atorvastatin blocks HMG-CoA reductase, a key enzyme in cholesterol synthesis; part of the drug resembles the enzyme’s natural substrate. Lisinopril blocks angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), which lowers blood pressure.

Her dentist adds a low dose of doxycycline for a few months. At that dose it does not act as an antibiotic; instead it slows the collagen-destroying enzymes (collagenases) in her gums. These enzymes need a zinc ion to work, and doxycycline binds metal ions. Before starting the statin, her nurse practitioner also checked her ALT, a liver enzyme measured in the blood.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Pharmacist

Many of the most-prescribed drugs are enzyme inhibitors: statins block HMG-CoA reductase, and ACE inhibitors and many antibiotics work the same way. Pharmacists explain these mechanisms to patients every day.
