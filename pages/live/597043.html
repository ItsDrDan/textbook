Before you start. You should be able to classify alcohols as 1°, 2° or 3° and predict what oxidation of each gives (Chapter 4).

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. Is 2-propanol a 1°, 2° or 3° alcohol?

    Answer
    2°: the carbon bearing the OH is bonded to two other carbons. If you missed it, review Section 4.2.

  2. What does oxidation of a 2° alcohol give?

    Answer
    A ketone (2-propanol gives propanone, acetone). If you missed it, review Section 4.5.

  3. Can a 3° alcohol be oxidized by the usual oxidizing agents?

    Answer
    No. The carbon bearing the OH has no hydrogen to lose. If you missed it, review Section 4.5.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 5 of 15

What the A1c measures.

Rosa asks her nurse practitioner why her A1c matters when her glucose meter readings are “usually fine.” The nurse explains that glucose in the blood is always switching between its ring form and a small amount of its open-chain form, which has an aldehyde group. That aldehyde can react with free amino groups on hemoglobin inside red blood cells, attaching the sugar permanently. Red blood cells live about four months (120 days), so the percentage of hemoglobin carrying glucose, the A1c, reflects average blood glucose over the past two to three months. An A1c of 8.4% corresponds to an average glucose of about 194 mg/dL.

Rosa’s mother remembers checking her own urine for sugar decades ago with tablets that turned from blue to orange-red.

Career Spotlight: Sterile Processing Technician

Sterile processing technicians clean and disinfect reusable instruments. High-level disinfectants such as glutaraldehyde and ortho-phthalaldehyde are aldehydes, and the reactivity of the C=O group is why they kill microbes.
