Your Route Through Chapter 9

Before you start. You should be able to write ester hydrolysis and saponification (Chapter 6), tell polar from nonpolar molecules (Chapter 1), and recognize cis and trans double bonds (Chapter 3).

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. What are the products of hydrolyzing an ester with NaOH?

    Answer
    A carboxylate salt and an alcohol (saponification). If you missed it, review Section 6.8.

  2. Is a long hydrocarbon chain polar or nonpolar? Does it dissolve in water?

    Answer
    Nonpolar; it does not dissolve in water. If you missed it, review Section 2.6.

  3. In a cis double bond, are the two chain parts on the same side or opposite sides?

    Answer
    The same side. If you missed it, review Section 3.2.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 9 of 15

The lipid panel.

Rosa’s nurse practitioner goes over her lipid panel: LDL 142 mg/dL, HDL 38 mg/dL and triglycerides 230 mg/dL. High triglycerides and low HDL are common in type 2 diabetes. She keeps Rosa on atorvastatin, which lowers the liver’s production of cholesterol, and suggests eating fish such as salmon or sardines twice a week for their omega-3 fatty acids.

Rosa asks why cholesterol is called “bad” when the nurse also said her body needs it.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Clinical Laboratory Scientist
