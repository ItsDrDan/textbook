Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 7 of 15

Numbing for a deep cleaning.

Rosa returns for scaling and root planing, a deep cleaning below the gum line. The hygienist numbs the area with lidocaine with epinephrine. Lidocaine contains an amide group and a tertiary amine nitrogen, and it is supplied as lidocaine hydrochloride, the ammonium salt. Rosa’s metformin is also sold as a hydrochloride salt.

One area near a badly inflamed pocket stays sensitive, so the hygienist waits and adds a little more anesthetic. She explains that inflamed tissue is more acidic than healthy tissue, and local anesthetics often work less well there.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page. Rosa is a fictional patient; her story is not medical advice.

Career Spotlight: Registered Dental Hygienist (Local Anesthesia)

In many states, including California, dental hygienists may give local anesthetic injections after additional training. Lidocaine and articaine are amides and benzocaine is an ester. The basic amine in lidocaine and articaine is why each is sold as a water-soluble hydrochloride salt; benzocaine’s aromatic amine is too weak a base, so it is used only topically.
