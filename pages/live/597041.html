Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 3 of 15

Reading the food label.

At her diabetes class, a dietitian shows Rosa how to read food labels. She explains that for decades many shortenings and margarines were made by partial hydrogenation: hydrogen gas was added across some of the C=C double bonds of a liquid vegetable oil to make it more solid. The process also turned some of the remaining cis double bonds into trans double bonds. Trans fats raise LDL cholesterol, and the FDA has since removed partially hydrogenated oils from the U.S. food supply, but Rosa still checks the labels on imported snacks.

The dietitian also asks whether anyone at home smokes. Rosa’s husband does. Tobacco smoke contains polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons such as benzo[a]pyrene, and smoking is one of the strongest risk factors for gum disease.

Career Spotlight: Registered Dental Hygienist

Pit-and-fissure sealants and tooth-colored composite fillings are polymers. The hygienist places a liquid monomer with C=C double bonds, and a curing light starts the addition polymerization that hardens it in seconds.
