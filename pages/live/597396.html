This page is for instructors who teach CHEM 3B (Introductory Organic and Biological Chemistry) at Fresno City College or who are considering this book for a similar course. Students do not need to read it.

What each kind of page is for

  • Section pages (for example 8.1–8.7) are the required reading. Most start with text adapted from an open textbook and edited for CHEM 3B, followed by a Lecture Connection block: the course’s emphasis, worked examples, Professor Tips, clinical boxes, Try It practice and a short Summary. “Lecture” in those blocks means the CHEM 3B course as described by the course outline, not one instructor’s slides.
  • Chapter Guides are short study pages: a chapter summary, a concept map, reaction tables, video lessons, worked examples, common mistakes and the running patient case (Rosa).
  • Practice Problems pages hold 60–80 original problems per chapter, with reasoned answers. Online the answers are behind “Answer” buttons; in print they move to an answer key at the end of each page.
  • Practice Games pages link the ItsDrDan study games. The games are free to play but are not openly licensed (all rights reserved); they are linked, not part of the book’s licensed text.
  • Chapter landing pages open with a “Your Route Through Chapter N” box: prerequisites, a three-question readiness check and a suggested study path.

Conventions used throughout

The book states one convention wherever textbooks differ, so students see the same answer on every page. The main ones:

  • ATP: 2.5 per NADH, 1.5 per FADH2, 10 per acetyl CoA; 30–32 ATP per glucose (32 with the malate–aspartate shuttle, 30 with the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle); 106 for palmitate; 7.3 kcal/mol for ATP hydrolysis.
  • “Cytosol” for the site of glycolysis and lipogenesis; three stages of catabolism.
  • Six traditional enzyme classes (translocases mentioned but not assessed); “cofactor” for inorganic ions and “coenzyme” for organic, usually vitamin-derived, helpers.
  • IUPAC names on exams, with common names for recognition; hydrolyzable and non-hydrolyzable lipids; point, insertion and deletion mutations as three separate terms.
  • Oligopeptide 2–20, polypeptide 21–40, protein more than 40 amino acids; ten essential amino acids, counting arginine.
  • No reaction mechanisms with curved arrows, no R/S, no E/Z names, no meso compounds, no pKa values or Henderson–Hasselbalch calculations. No Newman projections or chair flips (one chair drawing of cyclohexane illustrates that rings are not flat); a conformation is taught only as “the same molecule, not an isomer.” Acid strength is a ranking (carboxylic acid > phenol ≈ thiol > alcohol ≈ water) shown with the NaHCO3 and NaOH tests; at body pH, –COOH is written –COO− and amines –NH3+.

If your course uses a different convention, the Lecture Connection blocks and Summaries are the places to change it (see Adapting the book, below).

Supplements

  • Course Outline Alignment maps every item of the CHEM 3B outline of record to the sections that teach it.
  • Auto-graded homework for every chapter is available in LibreTexts ADAPT. The box at the top of each Practice Problems page names the chapter’s assignment: each student gets a random set of questions drawn from a larger bank, with three attempts per question and a hint after a wrong answer, and opens it from Canvas so the score reaches the gradebook. Every question is public in ADAPT under CC BY 4.0 (author Daniel Kwasniewski), so you can copy the assignments into your own ADAPT course.
  • A CHEM 3B lab manual written to accompany this book is in preparation. The “In the Lab” boxes already tie each chapter to the lab topics in the course outline; experiment numbers and links will be added when the manual is published.

Adapting the book

You may copy, remix and edit this book within its licenses (see About This Book). Pages written for this book are CC BY 4.0; pages that adapt The Basics of General, Organic, and Biological Chemistry or Fundamentals of General, Organic, and Biological Chemistry are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and must stay NonCommercial-ShareAlike in any remix. Every adapted page ends with an attribution note; please keep it, and add a line describing your own changes. The LibreTexts Remixer is the easiest way to build your own version.
