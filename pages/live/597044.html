Your Route Through Chapter 6

Before you start. You should be able to predict the oxidation products of 1° alcohols and aldehydes (Chapters 4–5) and write a neutralization (CHEM 3A).

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. What does complete oxidation of ethanol give?

    Answer
    Acetic acid (ethanoic acid), by way of acetaldehyde. If you missed it, review Section 4.5.

  2. What are the products when an acid reacts with NaOH?

    Answer
    A salt and water (neutralization). If you missed it, review neutralization from CHEM 3A; Section 6.4 applies it to carboxylic acids.

  3. Which functional group has a C=O with a hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon?

    Answer
    An aldehyde. If you missed it, review Section 5.1.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 6 of 15

An aspirin burn and acid attack.

A week before her next dental visit, a sore tooth kept Rosa awake, so she held an aspirin tablet against her gum. The hygienist finds a painful white patch there: an aspirin burn. Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is an acid, and pressed against soft tissue it damages the surface cells. The hygienist reminds her that aspirin is meant to be swallowed, not placed on the gums.

Rosa also mentions that an old bottle of aspirin in her cupboard smells like vinegar. Meanwhile, the hygienist explains that each time Rosa sips sweetened coffee, plaque bacteria ferment the sugar into lactic acid, and the pH at the tooth surface can drop below about 5.5, the point at which enamel starts to dissolve.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page. Rosa is a fictional patient; her story is not medical advice.

Career Spotlight: Pharmacist

Pharmacists counsel patients on how a drug is absorbed and how long it lasts. Aspirin is an ester, many “prodrugs” are esters that the body hydrolyzes into the active drug, and carboxylic acids change charge with pH, all from this chapter.
