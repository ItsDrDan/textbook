Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 14 of 15

An evening walk.

Rosa starts walking for 30 minutes after dinner. Working muscles take up glucose and break it down through glycolysis, the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain to make ATP; this uptake of glucose lowers her after-dinner readings.

Her pharmacist mentions a rare but serious side effect of metformin: lactic acidosis, a build-up of lactate in the blood. The risk is higher when the body cannot get enough oxygen to its tissues, for example in severe infection, heart failure or dehydration, or when the kidneys cannot clear metformin.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Respiratory Therapist

When the oxygen supply fails, the electron transport chain stops and ATP production collapses. Respiratory therapists treat hypoxia and carbon monoxide or cyanide exposure, problems that live in this chapter’s mitochondria.
