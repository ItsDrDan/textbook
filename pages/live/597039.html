Your Route Through Chapter 1

Before you start. You should be able to count valence electrons from the periodic table, tell ionic from covalent bonding, and use the electronegativity trend (CHEM 3A).

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. How many valence electrons does a nitrogen atom have?

    Answer
    5 (nitrogen is in group 15, also called 5A). If you missed it, review Section 1.1.

  2. Which bond is more polar, C–H or O–H?

    Answer
    O–H. The electronegativity difference between O and H is much larger than between C and H. If you missed it, review Section 1.4.

  3. Is NaCl held together by ionic or covalent bonding?

    Answer
    Ionic. Sodium transfers an electron to chlorine, giving Na+ and Cl− ions. If you missed it, review Section 1.2.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 1 of 15

Meet Rosa.

Rosa Delgado is 52 and manages a school cafeteria in Fresno County. Three years ago a routine blood test showed she has type 2 diabetes. Today she has two appointments: a check-up with her nurse practitioner in the morning and a cleaning with the dental hygienist in the afternoon. You will follow Rosa through every chapter of this book, because nearly every topic in CHEM 3B shows up somewhere in her care.

At the pharmacy, Rosa picks up her three medicines. The pharmacist reminds her to take metformin with meals and a full glass of water. Metformin is a small molecule with several N–H bonds and no large hydrocarbon part, and it dissolves easily in water. Atorvastatin, her cholesterol medicine, is a much larger molecule built mostly from carbon–carbon and carbon–hydrogen bonds, with only a few polar groups.

Rosa Delgado is a fictional patient created for this book. Her story simplifies real care to show the chemistry; it is not medical advice, and real treatment decisions follow current clinical guidelines and a patient’s own care team.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Pharmacy Technician

Pharmacy technicians prepare and label medications under a pharmacist’s supervision. Whether a drug dissolves in water or in fat, and how it is stored, comes straight from its bonds, polarity and intermolecular forces, the ideas in this chapter.
