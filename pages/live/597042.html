Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 4 of 15

Bad breath and mouthwash.

The hygienist measures deep pockets around several of Rosa’s teeth and notices strong bad breath. She explains that the bacteria living in those pockets break down proteins and release volatile sulfur compounds, mainly hydrogen sulfide and methanethiol, CH3SH.

Rosa uses a strong mouthwash that contains about 20% ethanol. Because her mouth is already dry, the hygienist suggests an alcohol-free rinse instead. One rinse she recommends contains thymol, a compound with an –OH group bonded directly to a benzene ring. Another works by oxidizing sulfur compounds into products that do not smell.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Infection Preventionist

Infection preventionists, often registered nurses, choose the disinfectants and hand rubs a hospital or clinic uses. Alcohol-based hand rubs and phenolic surface disinfectants are chemistry from this chapter.
