Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 13 of 15

A low blood sugar in the dental chair.

Two weeks after starting insulin, Rosa skips lunch before a 2 p.m. dental appointment. In the chair she becomes shaky, sweaty and confused. The hygienist checks her blood glucose: 62 mg/dL, which is hypoglycemia (below 70 mg/dL). Following the office’s emergency plan, she gives Rosa 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, four glucose tablets, and rechecks in 15 minutes: 104 mg/dL. The appointment continues after Rosa eats a snack.

Later her nurse practitioner reviews how insulin, glucagon and metformin control blood glucose, adjusts her glargine dose, and reminds her not to skip meals.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Diabetes Care and Education Specialist

These specialists, often nurses or dietitians, teach patients how insulin, meals and exercise move blood glucose up and down. Glycolysis, glycogen and gluconeogenesis are the pathways behind every piece of that advice.
