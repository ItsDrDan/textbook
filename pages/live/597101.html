Problem 4.46

Why can phenol, with six carbons, dissolve somewhat in water while hexane cannot?

Answer
Its OH group hydrogen bonds with water, so phenol is moderately soluble (about 8 g/100 mL) even though the ring is nonpolar. Its weak acidity (pKa about 10) contributes almost nothing to solubility in pure water, but it is why phenol dissolves completely in NaOH solution, as the ionic phenoxide salt.

  4. 2-ethoxybutane

Problem 4.48

Which alcohol gives only ONE alkene on dehydration, so Zaitsev's rule is not needed?

  1. 2-butanol
  2. 2-pentanol
  3. 3-pentanol
  4. 2-methyl-2-butanol
Answer
C. In 3-pentanol the OH carbon (C3) has two identical neighbors (C2 and C4), so removing an H from either gives the same product, 2-pentene. In A, B, and D the two neighbors are different, so two alkenes are possible and the more substituted one is major.

Problem 4.49

Which statement about the equilibrium CH3CH=CH2 + H2O \(\rightleftharpoons\) CH3CH(OH)CH3 is correct?

  1. Adding excess water favors the alkene.
  2. Removing water as it forms favors the alcohol.
  3. Concentrated acid and heat favor the alkene.
  4. The acid catalyst works only in the forward direction.
Answer
C. By Le Chatelier's principle, removing water and heating (dehydration is endothermic) both push the equilibrium toward the alkene. Adding water favors the alcohol (so A and B are reversed), and a catalyst speeds both directions equally (D).

Problem 4.50

Which compound is the most acidic?

  1. cyclohexanol
  2. benzyl alcohol
  3. phenol
  4. ethanol
Answer
C. Only phenol has its OH attached directly to the aromatic ring, which delocalizes the negative charge of the phenoxide ion (pKa about 10). Benzyl alcohol has a CH2 between the ring and the OH, so it is an ordinary alcohol (pKa about 16), like A and D.

Problem 4.51

Compare and contrast ethanol and ethanethiol (CH3CH2SH): (a) which has the higher boiling point and why; (b) what each gives on oxidation; (c) which is more acidic.

Answer
(a) Ethanol (78 °C) boils far above ethanethiol (35 °C) even though the thiol is heavier, because O–H groups hydrogen bond strongly and S–H groups barely do. (b) Ethanol, a 1° alcohol, is oxidized at carbon to acetaldehyde and then acetic acid; ethanethiol is oxidized at sulfur, two molecules joining to form diethyl disulfide, CH3CH2S–SCH2CH3. (c) Ethanethiol (pKa about 10.6) is more acidic than ethanol (about 16) because the larger sulfur atom spreads out the negative charge of the thiolate ion.

Problem 4.52

Predict the major product, and explain your choice, when each is heated with concentrated H2SO4: (a) 2-methyl-3-pentanol; (b) 1-methylcyclohexanol.

Answer
(a) The OH is on C3, with neighbors C2 (which carries the methyl branch) and C4. Removing H from C2 gives 2-methyl-2-pentene, whose double bond carries three alkyl groups; removing H from C4 gives 4-methyl-2-pentene (numbered from the other end), whose double bond carries only two. Major: 2-methyl-2-pentene (Zaitsev). (b) The OH carbon (C1) is bonded to the methyl group and to two ring carbons. Forming the double bond inside the ring gives 1-methylcyclohexene (trisubstituted); forming it to the methyl carbon gives methylenecyclohexane (disubstituted). Major: 1-methylcyclohexene.

The table lists four compounds of nearly the same molar mass. Use it to answer the questions.

More Practice: Clinical and Integrative

Problem 4.55

A patient is admitted after drinking windshield-washer fluid (methanol). The emergency team gives fomepizole, an inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase. (a) Write the two-step oxidation of methanol and name both products. (b) Explain, using the chemistry of 1° alcohol oxidation, why blocking the first step protects the patient. (c) Why would ethanol also work as a (less convenient) antidote?

Answer
(a) CH3OH → HCHO (formaldehyde, methanal) → HCOOH (formic acid, methanoic acid); the standard 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid sequence. (b) Methanol itself is only mildly toxic; the aldehyde and especially formic acid cause the metabolic acidosis and optic nerve damage. If the enzyme cannot carry out the first oxidation, neither toxic product forms and the unchanged methanol is slowly excreted. (c) Ethanol is also a 1° alcohol and a substrate for the same enzyme. Given in enough quantity it occupies alcohol dehydrogenase as a competitive substrate, so methanol is oxidized much more slowly. (Concept only; dosing belongs to pharmacology.)

Problem 4.56

A dental hygienist recommends xylitol gum to a patient with early dental caries (tooth decay). Xylitol is HOCH2(CHOH)3CH2OH. (a) Classify each OH-bearing carbon in xylitol as 1° or 2°. (b) What term describes a molecule with this many OH groups, and what does that predict about its water solubility? (c) In one sentence, why does replacing sucrose with xylitol lower the risk of caries?

Answer
(a) The two end carbons (CH2OH) are 1°; the three middle carbons (CHOH) are each bonded to two carbons and are 2°. (b) A polyol (sugar alcohol); five OH groups on five carbons means extensive hydrogen bonding with water, so it is very soluble, like glycerol. (c) Plaque bacteria cannot ferment xylitol to acid, so the plaque pH does not drop below the critical value (about 5.5) at which enamel hydroxyapatite begins to demineralize.

Problem 4.57

Two mouthrinses are on a dental clinic shelf: one lists thymol (a phenol) and eucalyptol (an ether) as active ingredients; the other lists chlorhexidine. A patient asks why the first one "stings" and tastes bitter. Using what you know about phenols, give the chemical part of the answer, and state which functional-group test from this chapter would respond to thymol but not to eucalyptol.

Answer
Thymol has an OH bonded directly to an aromatic ring, so it is a phenol: a weak acid that readily crosses cell membranes and denatures proteins, which is both its antiseptic action and the reason it irritates oral tissue. Eucalyptol is an ether, with no O–H, so it is neither acidic nor protein-denaturing in the same way. The ferric chloride test (a colored complex with Fe3+) or solubility in NaOH would respond to thymol but not to the ether.

Problem 4.58

Integrative (Chapters 3 and 4). Starting from 1-butene, show how you could prepare butanone (CH3COCH2CH3) in two steps. Name the reaction type and the reagent or conditions for each step, and explain why 1-butanol would not be a useful intermediate.

Answer
Step 1, hydration (addition): CH2=CHCH2CH3 + H2O, with H+ catalyst → 2-butanol. Markovnikov's rule puts the H on the CH2 carbon (the one with more H already) and the OH on C2. Step 2, oxidation: 2-butanol, a 2° alcohol, with [O] (for example, chromate) → butanone, a ketone. 1-Butanol would be a 1° alcohol; its oxidation gives butanal and then butanoic acid, never the ketone, and hydration of 1-butene does not produce it in any case.

Problem 4.59

Integrative (Chapters 4 and 10 preview). Hair keratin is held in shape partly by disulfide bonds between cysteine residues (each contains an SH side chain). A permanent wave first applies a reducing agent, then a shaping step, then an oxidizing agent. Using thiol chemistry, explain what happens to the sulfur atoms in each chemical step.

Answer
Reduction ([H]) breaks each R–S–S–R disulfide bond into two R–SH thiol groups, so the protein chains are free to slide past one another while the hair is wrapped on rollers. Oxidation ([O]) then joins pairs of thiol groups, now in new positions, into new disulfide bonds (2 R–SH → R–S–S–R), locking in the new shape. The same reversible thiol/disulfide pair appears in the reaction 2 CH3CH2SH \(\rightleftharpoons\) CH3CH2SSCH2CH3 from this chapter.

Problem 4.62

List every intermolecular force present between (a) two molecules of 1-propanol, (b) two molecules of methanethiol, (c) a molecule of diethyl ether and a molecule of water, (d) two molecules of diethyl ether. Which pair is attracted most strongly?

Answer
(a) Dispersion, dipole–dipole, and hydrogen bonding (each molecule has an H on O). (b) Dispersion and dipole–dipole only; the S–H bond is barely polar and does not hydrogen bond. (c) Dispersion, dipole–dipole, and hydrogen bonding: water donates its H to the ether oxygen's lone pair. (d) Dispersion and dipole–dipole only; the ether has no H on its oxygen to donate. The 1-propanol pair (a) is attracted most strongly, which is why 1-propanol boils at 97 °C while diethyl ether, which is heavier, boils at 35 °C.

Problem 4.63

Write and balance the equation for the complete combustion of 1-butanol, C4H9OH.

Answer
C4H9OH + 6 O2 → 4 CO2 + 5 H2O. Carbon: 4 = 4. Hydrogen: 10 = 10. Oxygen: 1 (in the alcohol) + 12 = 13 on the left; 8 + 5 = 13 on the right. The alcohol's own oxygen atom is the one students most often forget.

Problem 4.64

When methanol burns completely in air, the products are

  1. formaldehyde and water
  2. carbon dioxide and water
  3. carbon dioxide and hydrogen gas
  4. formic acid and water
Answer
B. Combustion of any alcohol gives CO2 and H2O: 2 CH3OH + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 4 H2O. Formaldehyde and formic acid (A and D) are the products of oxidation with [O] or the liver's enzymes, not of burning.

Problem 4.65

Starting from 2-pentanol, choose the reagent or conditions that give each product: (1) 2-pentanone; (2) 2-pentene; (3) CO2 + H2O. Choices: [O]; H+, heat; O2, flame.

Answer
(1) [O]: 2-pentanol is a 2° alcohol, so oxidation gives the ketone 2-pentanone and stops. (2) H+, heat: dehydration removes H and OH from neighboring carbons; 2-pentene (two alkyl groups on the C=C) is the Zaitsev major product rather than 1-pentene. (3) O2, flame: combustion.

Problem 4.66

True or false? If false, correct the statement. (a) Dehydration of an alcohol is an addition reaction. (b) Oxidation of a 1° alcohol and reduction of an aldehyde are the same reaction run in opposite directions. (c) Hydration of an alkene and dehydration of an alcohol are reverse reactions.

Answer
(a) False. Dehydration removes H and OH from an alcohol and forms a double bond; it is an elimination reaction. (b) True. [O] converts the 1° alcohol to the aldehyde; [H] converts the aldehyde back to the 1° alcohol (Chapter 5). (c) True. Both are catalyzed by H+; water adds in one direction and is removed in the other, and Le Chatelier's principle decides which way the equilibrium runs.

Problem 4.67

Fill in the blanks. The dehydration of 2-methyl-2-butanol can give two alkenes. By ________ rule, the major product is the ________ substituted alkene, ________, because its C=C carries ________ alkyl groups; the minor product is ________.

Answer
By Zaitsev's rule, the major product is the more substituted alkene, 2-methyl-2-butene, because its C=C carries three alkyl groups; the minor product is 2-methyl-1-butene (two alkyl groups on the C=C).

Problem 4.68

For each pair, decide whether the two are the same molecule, positional isomers, skeletal isomers, or functional group isomers: (a) CH3CH2CH2CH2OH and HOCH2CH2CH2CH3; (b) 2-pentanol and 3-pentanol; (c) 2-methyl-2-butanol and 1-pentanol; (d) 2-methoxypropane and 2-butanol; (e) 1-propanethiol and 2-propanethiol.

Problem 4.70

A student writes: “Oxidizing 2-methyl-2-propanol, (CH3)3COH, gives a ketone.” Find the error and correct it.

Answer
2-Methyl-2-propanol is a 3° alcohol: the carbon bearing the OH is bonded to three carbons and has no H. Oxidation to a C=O requires removing an H from that carbon, so a 3° alcohol is not oxidized by the usual oxidizing agents. Only 2° alcohols give ketones (and 1° alcohols give aldehydes, then acids). Cumulative Problem 4.1 (Chapters 1 and 4)

Ethanol (CH3CH2OH, 46 g/mol), dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3, 46 g/mol) and propane (CH3CH2CH3, 44 g/mol) have almost the same mass. Rank them from highest to lowest boiling point and name the strongest intermolecular force in each.

  1. ethanol > dimethyl ether > propane; hydrogen bonding, dipole–dipole, dispersion
  2. propane > dimethyl ether > ethanol; dispersion in all three
  3. dimethyl ether > ethanol > propane; dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding, dispersion
  4. all three boil within a few degrees because their masses are the same
Answer
A. Ethanol has an O–H bond, so its molecules hydrogen bond (bp 78 °C). Dimethyl ether has polar C–O bonds but no O–H, so only dipole–dipole attraction (bp −25 °C). Propane is nonpolar: dispersion only (bp −42 °C). Same mass, a 120-degree spread in boiling point: the functional group, not the mass, is doing the work. This is why ethers are volatile anesthetics and alcohols are not.

Cumulative Problem 4.2 (Chapters 2, 3 and 4)

Propene is hydrated with water and an acid catalyst. (a) Draw the major product and name it. (b) Is it a 1°, 2° or 3° alcohol? (c) What is formed when it is oxidized, and what is that product’s name?

Answer
(a) Markovnikov addition (Chapter 3): the H goes to the CH2 end and the OH to the middle carbon, giving CH3CH(OH)CH3, 2-propanol (isopropyl alcohol, rubbing alcohol). (b) The carbon holding the OH is bonded to two other carbons, so it is a 2° alcohol. (c) A 2° alcohol oxidizes to a ketone: CH3COCH3, propanone (acetone), and it stops there because a ketone has no H on the carbonyl carbon to lose.

Cumulative Problem 4.3 (Chapters 2, 3 and 4)

2-Butanol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid, and the alkene that forms is then hydrogenated over platinum. (a) Name the reaction in each step. (b) Draw and name the major alkene from step 1 and explain why it is major. (c) Name the final product.

Answer
(a) Step 1 is dehydration (an elimination of water); step 2 is hydrogenation (an addition). (b) Losing water can remove an H from C1 or from C3. Removing it from C3 gives 2-butene (cis and trans), the more substituted alkene, which is more stable and is the major product (Zaitsev’s rule); 1-butene is minor. (c) Hydrogenating either butene gives butane. Two steps turn an alcohol into an alkane.

Cumulative Problem 4.4 (Chapters 1 and 4)

1-Butanol dissolves in water to about 8 g per 100 mL; 1-octanol barely dissolves at all. Both have the same functional group. Explain. Then explain why diethyl ether, once a common surgical anesthetic, was so dangerous in the operating room.

Answer
The –OH group hydrogen bonds with water and pulls the molecule into solution; the hydrocarbon chain is nonpolar and pushes it out. With four carbons the OH still wins; with eight carbons the nonpolar tail dominates and the alcohol is almost insoluble. (Rule of thumb from Chapter 4: one OH can “carry” about four or five carbons into water.) Diethyl ether, CH3CH2OCH2CH3, cannot hydrogen bond with itself, so it boils at only 35 °C: it evaporates readily at room temperature, its vapor is heavier than air and extremely flammable, and a spark from equipment could ignite it. Modern inhaled anesthetics are halogenated ethers that are not flammable.

Cumulative Problem 4.5 (Chapters 2 and 4)

Name CH3CH(OH)CH(CH3)CH3 and classify it. Then draw a constitutional isomer of it that is a tertiary alcohol and one that is an ether, and name both.

Answer
The longest chain containing the C–OH carbon has four carbons, OH on C2 and a methyl on C3: 3-methyl-2-butanol, a 2° alcohol (the OH carbon is attached to two carbons). A tertiary isomer, formula C5H12O: 2-methyl-2-butanol, CH3C(OH)(CH3)CH2CH3, whose OH carbon is attached to three carbons. An ether isomer: ethyl propyl ether, CH3CH2OCH2CH2CH3 (or methyl butyl ether). Same formula, three different families: the 2° alcohol oxidizes to a ketone, the 3° alcohol does not oxidize at all, and the ether has no O–H to oxidize and boils far lower than either alcohol.

Cumulative Problem 4.6 (Chapter 4)

In the clinic. A patient who drank windshield-washer fluid (methanol) is treated in the emergency department with intravenous ethanol or with the drug fomepizole. Methanol itself is only mildly toxic; the damage is done by what the liver enzyme alcohol dehydrogenase turns it into. (a) Methanol is a 1° alcohol. What does it oxidize to, in two steps? Give names and structures. (b) Why does giving ethanol help?

Answer
(a) A 1° alcohol oxidizes first to an aldehyde and then to a carboxylic acid: methanol, CH3OH, → methanal (formaldehyde), H2C=O, → methanoic acid (formic acid), HCOOH. Formic acid is what damages the optic nerve and causes the acidosis. (b) Alcohol dehydrogenase also oxidizes ethanol, and it prefers it. Flooding the enzyme with ethanol keeps methanol from being oxidized long enough for the kidneys to remove it unchanged. This is competitive inhibition, which you will study in Chapter 11.
