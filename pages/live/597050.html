Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 12 of 15

Adding insulin.

A year later, Rosa’s A1c is 8.1%, still above her goal, so her nurse practitioner adds a once-daily, long-acting insulin called insulin glargine. Rosa is surprised to learn that her insulin is made by microorganisms. The human insulin gene, or an engineered version of it, is inserted into bacteria or yeast using recombinant DNA, and the cells produce the protein.

Insulin glargine differs from human insulin by three amino acids: a glycine replaces an asparagine at the end of one chain, and two arginines are added to the other. These changes make it release slowly from the injection site, and they were made by changing the DNA, not the protein.

Career Spotlight: Genetic Counselor

Genetic counselors help families understand genetic test results, from sickle cell trait to BRCA variants. Their work rests on how DNA is copied, read and changed by mutation.
