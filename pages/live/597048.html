Your Route Through Chapter 10

Before you start. You should be able to recognize amines, carboxylic acids and amides (Chapters 6–7) and relate pH to whether a group holds or loses H+.

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. At pH 7, is a carboxylic acid group mostly –COOH or –COO−?

    Answer
    –COO−. A carboxylic acid loses its H+ at body pH. If you missed it, review Section 6.10.

  2. At pH 7, is a typical amine group mostly –NH2 or –NH3+?

    Answer
    –NH3+. An amine picks up H+ at body pH. If you missed it, review Section 7.3.

  3. What bond forms when an amine reacts with a carboxylic acid (with heat)?

    Answer
    An amide bond. If you missed it, review Section 7.6.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 10 of 15

Protein in the urine and damaged gums.

A yearly urine test shows a small amount of albumin in Rosa’s urine: a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 45 mg/g, where under 30 is normal. Albumin is a globular protein that healthy kidneys keep in the blood. A little leaking through is an early warning sign of kidney damage from diabetes and high blood pressure; her lisinopril helps protect the kidneys.

At the dental office, the hygienist explains that in periodontitis, enzymes from bacteria and from Rosa’s own immune cells break down the collagen fibers that hold her teeth in their sockets.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page. Rosa is a fictional patient; her story is not medical advice.

Career Spotlight: Respiratory Therapist

Respiratory therapists manage oxygen delivery and read blood gases. Oxygen rides on hemoglobin, a protein whose four-subunit quaternary structure, and one amino acid change in sickle cell disease, you will study here.
