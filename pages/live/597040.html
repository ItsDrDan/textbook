Your Route Through Chapter 2

Before you start. You should be able to draw Lewis structures in which carbon makes four bonds and explain London dispersion forces (Chapter 1).

Readiness check. Try these before reading. If you miss one, review the material named above before you start.

  1. How many bonds does carbon make in a neutral molecule?

    Answer
    Four. If you missed it, review Section 1.1.

  2. In the Lewis structure of CH4, how many bonding pairs and lone pairs are on carbon, and what is the shape?

    Answer
    Four bonding pairs and no lone pairs; tetrahedral, with bond angles of about 109.5°. If you missed it, review Section 1.5.

  3. Which has the higher boiling point, CH4 or C4H10? Why?

    Answer
    C4H10. The larger molecule has stronger London dispersion forces. If you missed it, review Section 1.8.

Meet the Patient: Rosa’s Story, Part 2 of 15

Dry lips and a gas stove.

Diabetes often leaves the mouth dry, and Rosa’s lips are cracked. Before the cleaning, the hygienist coats them with petroleum jelly (petrolatum), a mixture of large alkanes with roughly 25 or more carbon atoms. Rosa jokes that her lips now feel “waterproof.”

That evening, Rosa’s mother, who lives with her, uses home oxygen through a nasal cannula. Her home-health nurse has warned the family never to use petroleum jelly on the mother’s nose or lips and to use a water-based moisturizer instead. In the kitchen, Rosa cooks dinner on a gas stove that burns methane, and a carbon monoxide detector hangs on the wall.

Keep Rosa in mind as you read. Her questions for this chapter are in the Clinical Update on the Chapter Guide page.

Career Spotlight: Anesthesia Technician

Anesthesia technicians set up and maintain the equipment that delivers inhaled anesthetics. Most modern inhaled anesthetics, such as sevoflurane and desflurane, are small halogenated hydrocarbons, the compounds at the end of this chapter.
