Learning Objectives

  • Describe how fatty acids are synthesized and compare to fatty acid break-down.

Lipogenesis or the synthesis of fatty acids occurs in the cytosol and endoplasmic reticulum of the cell and is chemically similar to the beta-oxidation process, but with a couple of key differences. The first of these occurs in preparing substrates for the reactions that grow the fatty acid. Transport of acetyl-CoA from the mitochondria occurs when it begins to build up. Two molecules can play roles in moving it to the cytosol – citrate and acetylcarnitine. Joining of oxaloacetate with acetyl-CoA in the mitochondrion creates citrate which moves across the membrane, followed by action of citrate lyase in the cytosol of the cell to release acetyl-CoA and oxaloacetate. Additionally, when free acetyl-CoA accumulates in the mitochondrion, it may combine with carnitine and be transported out to the cytosol.

The fatty acid chain is built two carbons at a time (opposite of beta-oxidation), beginning with two acetyl-CoA molecules. One is converted to malonyl-CoA by carboxylation, catalyzed by the enzyme acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC), the only regulatory enzyme of fatty acid synthesis. Next, both acetyl-CoA and malonyl-CoA have their CoA portions replaced by a carrier protein known as ACP (acyl carrier protein) to form acetyl-ACP and malonyl-ACP. Joining acetyl-ACP with malonyl-ACP releases the CO2 that was added and gives a four-carbon chain, acetoacetyl-ACP (see the figure in the Lecture Connection below).

From this point forward, the chemical reactions resemble those of beta oxidation reversed. First, the ketone is reduced to a hydroxyl using NADPH. Next, water is removed from carbons 2 and 3 of the hydroxyl intermediate to produce a trans double-bonded molecule. Last, the double bond is hydrogenated to yield a saturated intermediate. The process cycles with the addition of another malonyl-ACP to the growing chain until a 16-carbon chain is reached and released as palmitate. At this point, the cytosolic synthesis ceases.

Enzymes of Fatty Acid Synthesis

Acetyl-CoA carboxylase, which makes malonyl-CoA, is the main regulated enzyme of fatty acid synthesis. Insulin activates it; glucagon and epinephrine turn it off by adding a phosphate group (covalent modification). Citrate, a sign that the cell has plenty of fuel, activates it, and palmitoyl-CoA, a product of the pathway, inhibits it (feedback inhibition).

In animals, all the remaining enzyme activities that build palmitate are part of one large protein complex called fatty acid synthase.

Elongation of Fatty Acids

Fatty acids longer than 16 carbons are made in the endoplasmic reticulum, where enzymes add two more carbons at a time, again using malonyl-CoA.

Desaturation of Fatty Acids

Fatty acids are synthesized in the saturated form and desaturation occurs later. Enzymes called desaturases catalyze the formation of cis double bonds in mature fatty acids. These enzymes are found in the endoplasmic reticulum. Animals are limited in the desaturated fatty acids they can make, due to an inability to catalyze reactions beyond carbons 9 and 10. Thus, humans can make oleic acid, but cannot synthesize linoleic acid or linolenic acid. Consequently, these two must be provided in the diet and are referred to as essential fatty acids.

Lecture Connection

The lecture’s whole point about lipogenesis is in one sentence: it is not β-oxidation run backward. Different compartment (cytosol), different coenzyme (NADPH), different carrier (acyl carrier protein), a three-carbon malonyl donor, and the opposite hormone. Know the initial reactant and final product (acetyl CoA → palmitic acid, 16 C), classify the four elongation steps, and be able to line them up against β-oxidation.

Professor Tip

The fatty acid synthase complex, the D-configuration of the intermediate, elongases, desaturases, and the phosphorylation of acetyl CoA carboxylase are background; you are responsible for the steps and comparisons below. Remember that human desaturases cannot place a double bond beyond C9–C10 (counting from the carboxyl carbon), which is why linoleic and linolenic acids are essential (Chapter 9).

Getting started: acetyl ACP and malonyl ACP

When dietary intake provides more nutrients than energy requirements need, the liver turns the extra acetyl CoA into fatty acids and ships them to adipose tissue as VLDL (Section 15.1). Because acetyl CoA is made in the mitochondria, it must first be carried to the cytosol as citrate (the citrate–malate shuttle). The chain is built on acyl carrier protein (ACP), whose business end is the same pantothenic acid–thiol unit found in coenzyme A. Two starting complexes are needed. Acetyl ACP is made by transferring the acetyl group from acetyl CoA to ACP (a transferase). Malonyl ACP takes two steps: first acetyl CoA is carboxylated to the three-carbon malonyl CoA by acetyl CoA carboxylase (a ligase, using ATP and the coenzyme biotin; how can you tell? 2 C → 3 C and ATP → ADP), then the malonyl group is transferred to ACP. Malonyl CoA is the two-carbon donor for every round of chain elongation: it brings three carbons and loses one as CO2.

Each repetition adds two carbons: 4 C → 6 C → 8 C and so on, until the chain reaches 16 carbons (palmitic acid) and is released; seven rounds, 7 malonyl CoA, and 14 NADPH for one palmitate, a large energy investment. Longer fatty acids are made from palmitate by special enzymes that add C2 units, and unsaturated fatty acids are made by an oxidation step that requires O2. The NADPH comes largely from the pentose phosphate pathway (Section 13.6), which is why tissues that make a lot of fat, such as the liver, run that pathway hard.

Regulation. When blood glucose is high, insulin moves glucose into cells, stimulates glycolysis and pyruvate oxidation, and so supplies the acetyl CoA for fatty acid synthesis; insulin also activates acetyl CoA carboxylase, while glucagon and epinephrine inhibit it. At the same time malonyl CoA blocks transport of acyl groups into the mitochondria (the carnitine shuttle, Section 15.3), so the new fat is not immediately burned.

Practice: Acetyl CoA is carboxylated to malonyl CoA using ATP. Fill in the four columns.

Answer
Type: carboxylation (a CO2, supplied as bicarbonate, is added). How can I tell: 2 C became 3 C and ATP became ADP, so a bond was formed at the cost of ATP. Enzyme: acetyl CoA carboxylase, a ligase. Coenzyme: biotin carries the CO2; ATP supplies the energy.

Summary

  • Lipogenesis builds fatty acids two carbons at a time in the cytosol, using acetyl CoA, malonyl CoA (made by acetyl CoA carboxylase with biotin and ATP) and NADPH.
  • Its chain-building steps are condensation, reduction, dehydration and reduction. These mirror the steps of β-oxidation, but the compartment, carrier, coenzyme and hormone signal are all different, so it is not β-oxidation run backward.
  • The usual product is palmitate (16 C), which can be lengthened or desaturated; humans cannot place a double bond beyond C9, so linoleic and linolenic acids are essential.
  • Insulin turns lipogenesis on; glucagon and epinephrine turn it off.
