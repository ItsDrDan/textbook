Learning Objectives

  • Name and draw alkenes, alkynes, cycloalkenes, and substituted benzenes.
  • Decide whether an alkene can have cis/trans isomers, and label them.
  • Predict the products of addition reactions (hydrogenation, halogenation, hydrohalogenation, hydration), using Markovnikov's rule.
  • Recognize elimination reactions as the reverse of addition.
  • Explain how benzene differs from an ordinary alkene.

Key Ideas

1. Naming unsaturated hydrocarbons

  • Pick the longest chain that contains the double or triple bond.
  • Number from the end that gives the multiple bond the lowest number, even if a branch ends up with a higher number.
  • Change the ending: -ene (C=C) or -yne (C≡C). Put the number of the first carbon of the multiple bond in front of the parent: 2-pentene (also written pent-2-ene).
  • Rings: cyclohexene. C1 and C2 are the double-bond carbons. Number through the double bond toward the first substituent.

2. Cis/trans isomers

A C=C cannot rotate. If each double-bond carbon has two different groups, two isomers exist:

  • cis: the matching groups (usually the main chain) are on the same side.
  • trans: they are on opposite sides.

If either double-bond carbon carries two identical groups (such as =CH2 or =C(CH3)2), cis/trans isomers are not possible. Alkynes are linear, so they never have cis/trans isomers.

3. Addition reactions: "open the cabinet doors"

Professor Tip

Picture the double bond as two cabinet doors. Addition opens the doors: one piece of the reactant goes on each carbon, and the C=C becomes a C–C single bond.

Markovnikov's rule (for HX and H2O added to an unsymmetrical alkene): the H goes to the double-bond carbon that already has more H's. The X or OH goes to the other carbon.

Professor Tip

"Hydrogen wants to make the most friends." The new H joins the carbon that already has the most hydrogens.

4. Elimination: the reverse of addition

Elimination removes two pieces from neighboring carbons and creates a double bond: dehydrogenation (−H2), dehalogenation (−X2), dehydrohalogenation (−HX), and dehydration (−H2O). When more than one alkene is possible, the major product usually has the more substituted double bond (Zaitsev's rule).

5. Aromatic compounds

  • Benzene (C6H6) is drawn with alternating double bonds, but its electrons are spread evenly around the ring. That makes it unusually stable, and it does not undergo the addition reactions above.
  • Common names to know: toluene (methylbenzene), phenol (hydroxybenzene), aniline (aminobenzene), and benzoic acid.
  • For two substituents: ortho (1,2: next-door neighbors), meta (1,3: one carbon in between), para (1,4: directly opposite).

6. Polymers

Many alkenes (monomers) can add to one another over and over to make long addition polymers: polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC (from chloroethene), and Teflon (from tetrafluoroethene).

7. Benzene as a substituent and polynuclear aromatics

  • When a benzene ring is attached to a chain that holds the main functional group, it is named as a substituent: phenyl (C6H5–). Example: CH3CH(C6H5)CH2CH3 is 2-phenylbutane.
  • Polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) have two or more fused benzene rings: naphthalene (2 rings, mothballs), anthracene (3 rings). Larger PAHs such as benzo[a]pyrene form when organic matter burns incompletely and are carcinogens in tobacco smoke and charred food.

8. Benzene reacts by substitution, not addition

In electrophilic aromatic substitution an H on the ring is replaced, so the stable aromatic ring survives:

  • Halogenation: benzene + Br2 (FeBr3 catalyst) → bromobenzene + HBr
  • Nitration: benzene + HNO3 (H2SO4 catalyst) → nitrobenzene + H2O
  • Sulfonation: benzene + SO3 (H2SO4) → benzenesulfonic acid

Alkenes (and alkynes) also undergo combustion, just like alkanes: C2H4 + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 2 H2O.

Health Career Connection

  • Dental hygiene – tooth-colored fillings and sealants: Composite resins and pit-and-fissure sealants are liquid monomers with C=C double bonds (methacrylates such as Bis-GMA). When the curing light is applied, the double bonds "open" and link the monomers into a hard addition polymer inside the tooth. You are watching addition polymerization chairside.
  • Nursing – medical plastics: IV bags and tubing are often PVC. Syringes are usually polypropylene. Both are addition polymers of simple alkenes.
  • Nutrition – trans fats: Partial hydrogenation of vegetable oils converts some natural cis double bonds to trans. Trans fats raise LDL ("bad") cholesterol, which is why they now have to be listed on food labels. More in Chapter 9.
  • Pharmacology: Benzene rings appear in many drugs patients take every day, including aspirin, acetaminophen, and ibuprofen.

In the Lab

  • Experiment 3 – Reactions of Hydrocarbons
  • Bromine test: the red color of Br2 disappears quickly with an alkene (addition across the C=C). With an alkane the color lingers and fades only slowly in light (substitution, which also releases HBr). Benzene gives no reaction.
  • KMnO4 test: purple permanganate adds to a C=C to make a diol and turns into a muddy brown MnO2 solid. Alkanes and aromatic rings stay purple.
  • You will use these two tests to identify an unknown hydrocarbon as saturated or unsaturated.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Naming an alkene and checking for cis/trans

Name CH3CH=CHCH2CH(CH3)CH3. Can it exist as cis and trans isomers?

Solution

The longest chain containing the C=C has 6 carbons. Numbering from the left puts the double bond at C2 (from the right it would be C4), so number from the left. The methyl branch is on C5.

Name: 5-methyl-2-hexene. C2 has H and CH3, and C3 has H and CH2R. Each double-bond carbon has two different groups, so yes, cis and trans isomers exist.

Example 2: Markovnikov addition of HBr

Predict the major product of propene, CH2=CHCH3, + HBr.

Solution

C1 (=CH2) has 2 H's and C2 (=CH–) has 1 H. The H goes to C1 and the Br goes to C2.

Product: CH3CHBrCH3, 2-bromopropane (not 1-bromopropane).

Example 3: Hydration

Predict the product of 2-methylpropene, (CH3)2C=CH2, + H2O (H+ catalyst).

Solution

H goes to the =CH2 carbon. OH goes to the carbon holding the two methyl groups.

Product: (CH3)3C–OH, 2-methyl-2-propanol.

Example 4: Elimination (Zaitsev)

2-Bromobutane, CH3CHBrCH2CH3, undergoes dehydrohalogenation. What is the major alkene product?

Solution

HBr can be removed toward C1 (giving 1-butene) or toward C3 (giving 2-butene). 2-Butene has more carbon groups on its double bond, so it is the major product. Notice this is just Example 2's addition run backwards.

Example 5: Naming a disubstituted benzene

A benzene ring has Cl atoms on carbons 1 and 3. Give the IUPAC and ortho/meta/para names.

Solution

1,3-dichlorobenzene, also called m-dichlorobenzene (one carbon in between = meta).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Numbering to favor a branch instead of the double bond. The C=C always gets the lowest number.
  • Assigning cis/trans when one carbon of the double bond has two identical groups.
  • Using addition reactions on benzene. Aromatic rings resist addition.
  • Forgetting Markovnikov's rule. With symmetrical alkenes (2-butene) it does not matter, but with unsymmetrical alkenes it does.

Practice

Exercise 1

Name HC≡CCH(CH3)CH3.

Answer
3-methyl-1-butyne

Exercise 2

Which can have cis/trans isomers: (a) 1-pentene, (b) 2-pentene, (c) 2-methyl-2-pentene?

Answer
Only (b). In (a) C1 has two H's. In (c) C2 has two CH3 groups.

Exercise 3

Give the product of each: (a) cyclopentene + H2/Pt; (b) ethene + Cl2; (c) 1-butene + HCl.

Answer
(a) cyclopentane; (b) 1,2-dichloroethane; (c) 2-chlorobutane (Markovnikov)

Exercise 4

Draw (condensed) and name the para isomer of dimethylbenzene.

Answer
1,4-dimethylbenzene (p-dimethylbenzene, common name p-xylene): CH3–C6H4–CH3 with the two methyls directly across the ring.

Exercise 5

What monomer is used to make polypropylene?

Answer
Propene, CH2=CHCH3

Exercise 6

Give the organic product of benzene + Cl2 with an FeCl3 catalyst. What type of reaction is it?

Answer
Chlorobenzene (+ HCl). Electrophilic aromatic substitution: one ring H is replaced by Cl and the ring stays aromatic.
