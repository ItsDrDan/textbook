What this page is for

Drawing Lewis structures is the one skill from this chapter that every later chapter leans on: functional groups (Chapter 2 onward), polarity and hydrogen bonding (Section 1.8), acid–base behavior of amines and carboxylic acids, and the shapes of biomolecules all start from a correct Lewis structure. This page gives you unlimited practice with instant checking.

Lewis Structure Studio

Draw a Lewis structure atom by atom, then let the game check your electron count, octets, formal charges, VSEPR shape and polarity. It is free, works on a phone, and keeps your streak in this browser. The method it checks is exactly the one in Section 1.3 (count valence electrons, connect atoms, complete octets, convert lone pairs to multiple bonds if needed) and Section 1.5 (shape and polarity).

Suggested order

  1. Warm up (octet rule): CH4, NH3, H2O, HF, CCl4.
  2. Multiple bonds: O2, N2, CO2, HCN, H2CO (formaldehyde), C2H4, C2H2.
  3. Organic molecules you will see all semester: CH3OH, CH3NH2, CH3CHO, CH3COOH, CH3OCH3.
  4. Then predict the shape around each central atom (tetrahedral, trigonal pyramidal, bent, trigonal planar, linear) and whether the molecule is polar. If your answer and the game’s disagree, redraw before looking at the explanation.

Open Lewis Structure Studio in a new tab (recommended on a phone or small screen).

Professor Tip

On the exam you will draw structures on paper with no checker. After each game round, redraw the same molecule from memory on paper, including lone pairs, and only then compare. Students who practice this way stop losing points for missing lone pairs on O and N, which is the most common Lewis-structure error in CHEM 3B.
