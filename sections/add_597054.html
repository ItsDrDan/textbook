Professor Tip

Memorize "carbon 4, nitrogen 3, oxygen 2, hydrogen and halogens 1." Unpaired electrons form bonds; the paired ones stay on the atom as lone pairs (non-bonding pairs). For C, N, O, and F with a full octet (every atom in this course except the rare carbocation), bonds plus lone pairs add to four, which is the quickest way to spot a missing lone pair in a drawing. Sulfur follows oxygen's pattern (2 bonds, 2 lone pairs), which is why thiols (R–SH) look just like alcohols.

When an atom breaks the pattern, it carries a charge

So far every atom has been neutral. Organic chemistry is full of charged atoms, and the same cheat sheet finds them. Compare the atom's bonds and lone pairs with its neutral pattern:

  • Electron deficient: more bonds / fewer lone pairs than usual → positive (+). Example: N with 4 bonds and 0 lone pairs (as in NH4+), or O with 3 bonds and 1 lone pair (as in H3O+).
  • Electron rich: fewer bonds / more lone pairs than usual → negative (−). Example: O with 1 bond and 3 lone pairs (as in OH− or CH3O−), or C with 3 bonds and 1 lone pair.

You will use this rule constantly: an amine nitrogen that accepts H+ becomes N+ with four bonds (Chapter 7), a carboxylic acid that loses H+ leaves an O− with one bond and three lone pairs (Chapter 6), and the lidocaine in a dental cartridge is sold as its N+ form because the charged ion dissolves in water.

Example 1: Reading a charge from bonds and lone pairs

Problem

(a) Hydronium ion, H3O+: the oxygen has three bonds and one lone pair. (b) Amide ion, NH2−: the nitrogen has two bonds and two lone pairs. Use the cheat sheet to confirm the sign of each charge.

Solution

(a) Neutral oxygen has 2 bonds and 2 lone pairs. This oxygen has one more bond and one fewer lone pair, so it is electron deficient: positive. (b) Neutral nitrogen has 3 bonds and 1 lone pair. This nitrogen has one fewer bond and one more lone pair, so it is electron rich: negative.

Check

Count electrons the long way. H3O+: 6 + 3(1) − 1 = 8 valence electrons = 3 bonds (6) + 1 lone pair (2). NH2−: 5 + 2(1) + 1 = 8 = 2 bonds (4) + 2 lone pairs (4). Both drawings use exactly the right number of electrons, so the charges are real, not drawing errors. ✓

Practice: draw methanol, methylamine, and chloromethane with every lone pair shown, then check each heavy atom against the cheat sheet. The Lewis Structure Studio game (Section 1.11, Practice Games, at the end of this chapter) will grade your drawings.

Answers to Exercises

Exercise 1.1.2

Both Cl and N form the expected number of bonds. Cl (group 7A) has one bond and 3 lone pairs. The central atom N (group 5A) has 3 bonds and one lone pair. Yes, the Lewis structure of NCl3 follows the octet rule.
