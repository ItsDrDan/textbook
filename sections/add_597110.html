Lecture Connection

This short section closes the oxidation ladder you started in Chapter 4: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid with [O], and the same ladder run backward with [H]. In lecture the ladder is the single best way to keep the alcohol, aldehyde and acid families connected.

The oxidation ladder

  • Making an acid: oxidation of a 1° alcohol. 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid with [O] (Chapter 4; you made these products in the alcohol oxidation lab). The section's example is the one your liver runs: ethanol → acetaldehyde → acetic acid, with alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase doing the oxidizing.
  • Reduction of a carboxylic acid runs the ladder backward with [H]: carboxylic acid → aldehyde → 1° alcohol. A ketone, by comparison, reduces only to a 2° alcohol, and a 2° alcohol never oxidizes past the ketone. So CH3CH2COOH + [H] gives propanal and then 1-propanol. In the lab a strong hydride reagent is needed; in the body enzymes do it.
  • A 3° alcohol sits off the ladder entirely: no H on its C–OH carbon, so no oxidation and no carbonyl to come from.

Example: Reduction of a carboxylic acid, and the oxidation ladder

Problem. (a) What are the products when 3-methylbutanoic acid is reduced, first partially and then completely, with [H]? (b) What 1° alcohol would you oxidize to make 3-methylbutanoic acid? (c) Could any reduction turn 3-methylbutanoic acid into a 2° or 3° alcohol?

Solution. (a) Reduction removes oxygen or adds hydrogen. The carboxylic acid first becomes the aldehyde, 3-methylbutanal, (CH3)2CHCH2CHO, and then the aldehyde becomes the 1° alcohol 3-methyl-1-butanol, (CH3)2CHCH2CH2OH. (b) The same alcohol, 3-methyl-1-butanol: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → acid with [O] is the Chapter 4 reaction run forward. (c) No. The carbon that carries the oxygen in a carboxylic acid is bonded to only one other carbon, and reduction never changes the carbon skeleton, so the alcohol must be 1°. 2° alcohols come from ketones.

Check. Write the ladder: 1° alcohol \(\rightleftharpoons\) aldehyde \(\rightleftharpoons\) carboxylic acid, with [O] going up and [H] coming down. The carbon count (five) is the same on every rung.

Professor Tip

Every rung of the ladder keeps the same carbon skeleton; only the oxidation state of one carbon changes. If a question gives you an acid and asks what alcohol it came from, keep every carbon, turn the COOH into CH2OH, and you have a 1° alcohol every time.
