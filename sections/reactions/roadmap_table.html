CombustionAn alkane burns in O2 to CO2 and H2O, releasing energy.Section 2.7
Halogenation (substitution)Light (hν) and X2: one H on the alkane is replaced by a halogen; HX is the by-product.Section 2.7
HydrogenationH2 with a Pt or Ni catalyst adds across the C=C; the alkene becomes an alkane.Section 3.4
Halogenation (addition)X2 adds across the C=C, one halogen on each carbon (a dihaloalkane).Section 3.4
HydrohalogenationHX adds across the C=C: H on one carbon, X on the other (an alkyl halide).Section 3.4
HydrationH2O with an acid catalyst adds across the C=C as H and OH, making an alcohol.Section 3.4
DehydrationH2SO4 and heat remove OH and an H from the neighboring carbon as H2O; a C=C forms (the reverse of hydration).Section 4.5
Oxidation of a 1° alcoholMild [O]: a primary alcohol becomes an aldehyde.Section 4.5
Oxidation of a 1° alcohol (excess [O])Excess oxidizing agent: primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid.Section 4.5
Oxidation of a 2° alcohol[O]: a secondary alcohol becomes a ketone. A tertiary alcohol does not react.Section 4.5
Oxidation of an aldehyde[O] (including Tollens’ and Benedict’s reagents): –CHO becomes –COOH. Ketones do not react.Section 5.4
ReductionH2 with Ni adds across the C=O: an aldehyde gives a 1° alcohol, a ketone gives a 2° alcohol.Section 5.4
Hemiacetal formationOne alcohol adds across the C=O; the carbon ends up with –OH and –OR.Section 5.3
Acetal formationTwo alcohols react with the C=O (acid catalyst); the carbon ends up with two –OR groups, and H2O is released.Section 5.3
EsterificationCarboxylic acid + alcohol, acid catalyst and heat: a condensation that makes an ester and H2O.Section 6.7
Acid hydrolysis of an esterWater and acid split the ester back into a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.Section 6.8
SaponificationNaOH and heat split the ester into a carboxylate salt and an alcohol.Section 6.8
Neutralization of a carboxylic acidA base (NaOH) removes the acidic H: carboxylate ion + water.Section 6.4
Amine as a baseAn amine accepts H+ from an acid: an ammonium ion (amine salt).Section 7.3
AmidationCarboxylic acid + amine (or NH3) with heat: a condensation that makes an amide and H2O.Section 7.6
Acid hydrolysis of an amideWater and acid break the C–N bond: carboxylic acid + ammonium ion.Section 7.7
Base hydrolysis of an amideNaOH breaks the C–N bond: carboxylate salt + amine (or NH3).Section 7.7
