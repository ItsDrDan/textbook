Lecture Connection

The lecture’s whole point about lipogenesis is in one sentence: it is not β-oxidation run backward. Different compartment (cytosol), different coenzyme (NADPH), different carrier (acyl carrier protein), a three-carbon malonyl donor, and the opposite hormone. Know the initial reactant and final product (acetyl CoA → palmitic acid, 16 C), classify the four elongation steps, and be able to line them up against β-oxidation.

Professor Tip

The fatty acid synthase complex, the D-configuration of the intermediate, elongases, desaturases, and the phosphorylation of acetyl CoA carboxylase are background; you are responsible for the steps and comparisons below. Remember that human desaturases cannot place a double bond beyond C9–C10 (counting from the carboxyl carbon), which is why linoleic and linolenic acids are essential (Chapter 9).

Getting started: acetyl ACP and malonyl ACP

When dietary intake provides more nutrients than energy requirements need, the liver turns the extra acetyl CoA into fatty acids and ships them to adipose tissue as VLDL (Section 15.5). Because acetyl CoA is made in the mitochondria, it must first be carried to the cytosol as citrate (the citrate–malate shuttle). The chain is built on acyl carrier protein (ACP), whose business end is the same pantothenic acid–thiol unit found in coenzyme A. Two starting complexes are needed. Acetyl ACP is made by transferring the acetyl group from acetyl CoA to ACP (a transferase). Malonyl ACP takes two steps: first acetyl CoA is carboxylated to the three-carbon malonyl CoA by acetyl CoA carboxylase (a ligase, using ATP and a biotin cofactor; how can you tell? 2 C → 3 C and ATP → ADP), then the malonyl group is transferred to ACP. Malonyl CoA is the two-carbon donor for every round of chain elongation: it brings three carbons and loses one as CO2.

Chain elongation: four reactions, repeated

Steps 2, 3, and 4 are the reverse of steps 3, 2, and 1 of β-oxidation. Set up the table:

Each repetition adds two carbons: 4 C → 6 C → 8 C and so on, until the chain reaches 16 carbons (palmitic acid) and is released; seven rounds, 7 malonyl CoA, and 14 NADPH for one palmitate, a large energy investment. Longer fatty acids are made from palmitate by special enzymes that add C2 units, and unsaturated fatty acids are made by an oxidation step that requires O2. The NADPH comes largely from the pentose phosphate pathway (Section 13.6), which is why tissues that make a lot of fat, such as the liver, run that pathway hard.

Regulation. When blood glucose is high, insulin moves glucose into cells, stimulates glycolysis and pyruvate oxidation, and so supplies the acetyl CoA for fatty acid synthesis; insulin also activates acetyl CoA carboxylase, while glucagon and epinephrine inhibit it. At the same time malonyl CoA blocks transport of acyl groups into the mitochondria (the carnitine shuttle, Section 15.1), so the new fat is not immediately burned.

Practice: Acetyl CoA is carboxylated to malonyl CoA using ATP. Fill in the four columns.

Answer
Type: carboxylation (a CO2, supplied as bicarbonate, is added). How can I tell: 2 C became 3 C and ATP became ADP, so a bond was formed at the cost of ATP. Enzyme: acetyl CoA carboxylase, a ligase. Coenzyme: biotin carries the CO2; ATP supplies the energy.

Summary

  • Lipogenesis builds fatty acids two carbons at a time in the cytosol, using acetyl CoA, malonyl CoA (made by acetyl CoA carboxylase with biotin and ATP) and NADPH.
  • Its chain-building steps are condensation, reduction, dehydration and reduction: roughly β-oxidation run in reverse.
  • The usual product is palmitate (16 C), which can be lengthened or desaturated; humans cannot place a double bond beyond C9, so linoleic and linolenic acids are essential.
  • Insulin turns lipogenesis on; glucagon and epinephrine turn it off.
