Molecule in the Clinic: Insulin

Insulin is a small protein of 51 amino acids in two chains: an A chain of 21 and a B chain of 30, linked by two disulfide bonds, with a third disulfide inside the A chain. Rapid-acting “insulin lispro” differs from human insulin only in that the amino acids at positions B28 and B29 (proline and lysine) are swapped, which makes it cluster less and absorb faster after injection.

Your turn: What bonds hold the A and B chains together, and which amino acid forms them?

Answer
Covalent disulfide bonds (–S–S–), formed between the side chains of two cysteine residues.

Your turn: Swapping two amino acids in lispro changes which level of protein structure directly?

Answer
The primary structure, the amino acid sequence. The change in how the molecules associate follows from it.
