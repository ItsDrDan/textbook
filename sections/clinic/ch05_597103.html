Molecule in the Clinic: Glutaraldehyde (a cold sterilant)

Glutaraldehyde is a high-level disinfectant used for instruments that cannot be heated in an autoclave, such as endoscopes. Its two aldehyde groups react with the amino groups of microbial proteins and cross-link them, which kills bacteria, viruses and spores.

Your turn: Give the IUPAC name of glutaraldehyde.

Answer
Pentanedial: five carbons, two aldehyde groups (the ending “-dial”), both on chain ends so no numbers are needed.

Your turn: Why must staff wear gloves and work with good ventilation when they use it?

Answer
The same reaction that cross-links microbial proteins also attacks the proteins of skin and airways, so glutaraldehyde irritates them and can cause allergic sensitization.
