Molecule in the Clinic: Halothane

Halothane, CF3CHBrCl, was a standard inhaled general anesthetic for decades. In the United States it has been replaced by newer halogenated anesthetics such as sevoflurane, but it is still used in some parts of the world.

Your turn: Give the IUPAC name of halothane.

Answer
2-bromo-2-chloro-1,1,1-trifluoroethane. Two carbons make it an ethane; the halogens are named as substituents in alphabetical order (bromo, chloro, fluoro), numbered so the set of locants is lowest.

Your turn: Why does a small halogenated hydrocarbon reach the brain so quickly?

Answer
It is small and has low polarity, so it dissolves easily in the nonpolar lipid interior of cell membranes, including the membranes of nerve cells.
