Molecule in the Clinic: Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

Acetaminophen is the most widely used medicine for pain and fever. Its structure explains how it behaves in water: a benzene ring that is nonpolar, plus a phenol O–H and an amide group (N–H and C=O) that are very polar.

Your turn: Which intermolecular forces can acetaminophen form with water? Name the hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors.

Answer
All three: dispersion forces, dipole–dipole forces and hydrogen bonds. The H on the O–H and the H on the N–H are donors; the O of the OH and the carbonyl O are acceptors. The nonpolar ring is why acetaminophen is only moderately soluble in water rather than very soluble.
