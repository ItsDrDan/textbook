Molecule in the Clinic: Propofol, the “milky” anesthetic

Propofol is the most common drug for starting general anesthesia and for procedural sedation. It is a phenol that barely dissolves in water, so it is given IV as a white oil-in-water emulsion of soybean oil, glycerol and egg lecithin. Because the emulsion is also a good food for bacteria, opened vials must be used promptly or discarded.

Your turn: Lecithin is a glycerophospholipid. How does it keep the oil droplets dispersed in water?

Answer
Its two fatty-acid tails dissolve in the oil droplet and its charged phosphate–choline head faces the water, coating each droplet with a polar surface so the droplets do not merge.
