Who this book is for

Introductory Organic and Biological Chemistry for FCC is a free, openly licensed textbook built for CHEM 3B at Fresno City College. It follows the course outline of record for CHEM 3B (effective Spring 2027, C-ID CHEM 102) chapter by chapter, and it was written with students headed into nursing, dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy and the other health professions in mind. You will meet the chemistry first and then see, in every chapter, where that chemistry shows up in a clinic, a pharmacy, a dental operatory or a lab result.

Version 1.0, November 2026. Remixed and written by Dr. Dan (Daniel Kwasniewski), Department of Chemistry, Fresno City College.

Free to use

This book is free to read online and free to download. Students are never required to purchase it. CHEM 3B sections that use it, together with the free lab manual and the free study tools, qualify as zero-textbook-cost sections under California Education Code section 66406.9. A printed copy may be offered at cost, but it is optional.

How this book is organized

Each chapter has three kinds of pages.

  • Section pages (for example 4.1–4.10) carry the core reading. Most reproduce a section of an open textbook (see Sources, below). Below the source text, each of these pages has a Lecture Connection: what the CHEM 3B lecture emphasizes about that topic, in the instructor's words, with worked examples, figures, tips, clinical connections and an “In this course” note wherever the source text uses a different convention from the one you will use on exams. Ten sections that were empty placeholders in the source books were written new for this course (4.10, 7.11, 11.7, 12.9, 13.7–13.10, 14.5 and 15.6).
  • Chapter Guide pages are the study guide for the chapter: learning objectives mapped to the course outline, short Key Ideas with pointers to the sections, Professor Tips, exam-style worked examples in Problem/Solution/Check form, common mistakes, video lessons from the instructor's CHEM 3B playlist, and interactive Try It practice.
  • Practice Problems and Practice Games pages close each chapter. The problems are original to this book and include multiple choice, compare-and-contrast, data analysis and clinical-application items, all with worked answers behind an “Answer” toggle. The games pages embed the free study games from Dr. Dan's Chemistry Study Hub that fit the chapter.

How to use it

  1. Read the section pages before lecture, including the Lecture Connection at the bottom of each one; use the Chapter Guide's Key Ideas to check what you should be able to do.
  2. Work the worked examples with the solution covered, then open it and compare.
  3. Do the Try It sets as you go. Progress is saved only in your browser; nothing is graded or tracked.
  4. Finish with the Practice Problems. If a problem is hard, the matching game on the Practice Games page gives you unlimited fresh versions of the same skill.

Professor Tip

The clinical boxes are not extra reading. Exam questions in a health-science organic course are increasingly written as scenarios, so practice translating a story about a patient into the functional group, property or pathway it is really asking about.

Sources and adaptation statement

This book is a remix. The section text comes from three open textbooks on LibreTexts, reproduced unchanged by transclusion, and the rest was written for this course. A section-by-section table of sources, with the title, author, link and license of each one, is in the back matter under Source Attributions by Section.

What was changed (adaptation log). Compared with the source texts, this book:

  • reorders the sections into the fifteen-chapter sequence of the CHEM 3B outline and adds a bonding-review chapter at the start;
  • appends a Lecture Connection block to every adapted section (lecture emphasis, worked examples, figures, Professor Tips, nursing and dental-hygiene connections, “In this course” convention notes); the source text itself is not edited;
  • replaces ten empty placeholder sections with new, original sections;
  • adds a Chapter Guide, a Practice Problems page and a Practice Games page to every chapter, all original;
  • adds original figures (lecture-slide figures, structure and reaction drawings, pathway diagrams) with text descriptions, and embeds the instructor's own videos and practice tools;
  • standardizes conventions used on CHEM 3B exams where the source books differ (for example ATP yields of 2.5 per NADH and 1.5 per FADH2, “cytosol” rather than “cytoplasm”, and the enzyme-class and inhibitor vocabulary used in lecture); each such difference is stated on the page where it occurs.

No content from commercial textbooks, test banks, homework sets or exams was used. All practice problems and examples are original.

Accessibility statement

This book is built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA. Every figure, including every drawn structure and pathway diagram, carries a text description for screen readers and a visible caption; tables have header rows and captions; headings follow a consistent outline; formulas are written as text, not images; meaning is never carried by color alone; links are descriptive; and hidden answers use a standard disclosure control that works with the keyboard and with screen readers. Every page was checked with an automated WCAG 2.1 A/AA scan (axe-core) before publication, and the dense lecture-slide figures have longer descriptions that give a screen-reader user the same information a sighted student gets. The Try It widgets and games are operated with ordinary buttons and text fields, so they work from the keyboard, and each one has an “Open in a new tab” link for a larger view; the games have no required timed mode.

If you find a barrier — a figure without a description, a control you cannot reach from the keyboard, a video without accurate captions — tell your instructor, or report it at github.com/ItsDrDan/textbook/issues, so it can be fixed in the book rather than worked around. Students who need materials in another format can also contact Fresno City College's Disabled Students Programs and Services.

A note on medical content

The clinical connections explain the chemistry behind things you will see in practice. They are written at the level of a first chemistry course, not as clinical guidance, and they do not give treatment decisions or doses. Your health-program courses and your clinical instructors are the authority on patient care.
