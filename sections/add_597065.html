The section above contrasts hexane with sodium chloride as "organic versus inorganic." In lecture the same comparison is read as molecular versus ionic, which is the comparison that actually predicts properties.

Professor Tip

Every hexane property listed above (low melting and boiling points, insoluble in water but soluble in gasoline, flammable, nonconducting) belongs to hexane because it is a molecular compound held together by weak intermolecular forces, not because it came from petroleum. Inorganic compounds can be molecular too (water, carbon dioxide, ammonia), and they share the low melting and boiling points and poor conductivity. On an exam, say "molecular" or "ionic," and reserve "organic" for "contains carbon." The full comparison table is in Section 1.2.

Two reminders from Chapter 1 that this chapter leans on: hexane is nonpolar, so dispersion forces are the only attraction between its molecules (Section 1.8), and "flammable" is the one property that makes a hydrocarbon dangerous to handle.

Summary

  • “Organic” means a compound of carbon; its typical properties come from being molecular.
  • Molecular compounds (most organic compounds, plus water and CO2) have low melting and boiling points and do not conduct; ionic compounds have high melting points and conduct when dissolved or melted.
  • On exams, compare molecular versus ionic, and reserve “organic” for “contains carbon.”
