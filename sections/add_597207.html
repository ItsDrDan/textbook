Lecture Connection

How a water-insoluble fat gets emulsified, hydrolyzed, repackaged into lipoproteins, and transported is covered in Section 15.1, and how it is stored and finally mobilized in Section 15.2; this page covers activation and the carnitine shuttle. The lecture treats fatty acid breakdown as three stages: (1) the fatty acid is activated by binding to coenzyme A, (2) it is transported into the mitochondrial matrix by the carnitine shuttle, and (3) it is repeatedly oxidized, the fatty acid spiral, to acetyl CoA, FADH2, and NADH. Stages 1 and 2 are here; the four reactions of stage 3, with their table, are in Section 15.4.

Activation and the carnitine shuttle

Activation. A carboxylic acid is unreactive, so the first job is to convert it into a thioester. Acyl CoA synthetase (a ligase) on the outer mitochondrial membrane joins the fatty acid to coenzyme A:

R–COO− + CoA–SH + ATP → R–CO–S–CoA + AMP + PPi

ATP is split to AMP, not ADP, and the pyrophosphate (PPi) is then hydrolyzed to two phosphates. Two high-energy bonds are spent, so we count the cost as 2 ATP-equivalents per fatty acid, no matter how long the chain.

The carnitine shuttle. Acyl CoA is too large to pass through the inner mitochondrial membrane to the matrix, so it needs a shuttle. Carnitine acyltransferase catalyzes both acyl transfers: on the outside it moves the acyl group from CoA onto carnitine, a small carrier molecule; acyl-carnitine crosses the membrane; and on the inside the same kind of enzyme gives the acyl group back to a mitochondrial CoA. Both transfers are transferase reactions. The shuttle is also a control point: malonyl CoA, the three-carbon unit used to build fatty acids (Section 15.6), blocks the transport of acyl groups into the matrix, so a cell that is making fat does not burn it at the same time.

Clinical Connection: Nursing

Carnitine and MCT oil. Patients with an inherited carnitine deficiency, or with a defect in carnitine acyltransferase, cannot move long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria. They do fine after a meal, when glucose is available, but become hypoglycemic, weak, and sometimes dangerously ill when they fast or get a fever, because the fat they mobilize cannot be burned and the liver cannot spare glucose. Part of the dietary management is medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) oil: 8- and 10-carbon fatty acids are small enough to enter the matrix without the shuttle, so they can be activated and oxidized inside. The same shortcut is why MCT oil appears in some tube-feeding and ketogenic-diet formulas. A nurse who knows where the shuttle sits can explain why skipping meals is the danger, not the fat itself.

Summary

  • Fatty acids reach the cells from digested dietary fat carried in lipoproteins (Section 15.1) or from mobilized fat stores (Section 15.2).
  • Before it can be oxidized, a fatty acid is activated: it is joined to coenzyme A at the outer mitochondrial membrane, at a cost of the equivalent of 2 ATP (ATP → AMP).
  • The carnitine shuttle carries the acyl group into the mitochondrial matrix, where β-oxidation takes place.
