Lecture Connection

Lecture starts the chapter with two formulas and one word: CnH2n+2 for an open chain, CnH2n for a ring, and saturated. Knowing the saturated hydrogen count lets you tell at a glance whether a formula hides a ring or a double bond.

Alkane formulas and the saturation test

  • Open-chain (acyclic) alkanes: CnH2n+2
  • Cycloalkanes (one ring): CnH2n. Closing the ring costs two hydrogens.

Hydrocarbons are composed only of carbon and hydrogen, and they come in three shapes: straight-chain (the "n-" or normal alkanes), branched (like trees), and cyclic (rings, drawn as polygons). Acyclic simply means "not a ring." Alkanes and cycloalkanes are saturated hydrocarbons: every carbon has four single bonds, so the molecule is saturated with hydrogen and holds the maximum number of hydrogens its skeleton allows. A hydrocarbon with a C=C or C≡C bond is unsaturated; it has fewer hydrogens than the saturated formula predicts and, as you will see in Chapter 3, it can take up more atoms by addition. A quick test: an open-chain compound whose formula fits CnH2n+2 is saturated; every pair of missing hydrogens means one ring or one double bond.

Example 1: What does the formula tell you?

Problem

Three hydrocarbons have the formulas C7H16, C7H14, and C7H12. Which is a saturated open-chain alkane? What can you say about the other two?

Solution

For seven carbons the saturated open-chain count is 2(7) + 2 = 16 H, so C7H16 is the alkane (heptane or one of its branched isomers). C7H14 is two hydrogens short: it is either a cycloalkane (Section 2.9) or an alkene with one C=C (Chapter 3). C7H12 is four hydrogens short: two rings, two double bonds, one of each, or one triple bond.

Check

Each pair of missing hydrogens means one ring or one double bond, and a triple bond counts as two pairs. The formula alone cannot say which, so when a question gives only a formula, draw a structure before you answer. ✓

The parent names for one to ten carbons (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, then the Greek numbers pent- through dec-) are the vocabulary for every name in the rest of the course; the table of parents and alkyl groups is in Section 2.5.
