Lecture Connection

In lecture, redox for carbonyls comes down to two rules and two test tubes. Rules: aldehydes oxidize to carboxylic acids, ketones do not; [H] reduces an aldehyde to a 1° alcohol and a ketone to a 2° alcohol. Test tubes: Tollens' reagent (silver mirror) and Benedict's reagent (blue to brick red), both of which say "aldehyde present."

Professor Tip

Use these definitions on exams: an oxidation is an increase in C–O bonds and/or a decrease in C–H bonds; a reduction is the reverse. Write [O] for any oxidizing agent and [H] for any reducing agent (H2 with a Ni, Pd or Pt catalyst, or NaBH4). The alcohol oxidations and the thiol/disulfide pair reviewed here are Chapter 4 material (Sections 4.5 and 4.9); the NAD+/NADH coenzyme returns in Chapter 13.

Oxidation: aldehydes yes, ketones no

  • Aldehyde → carboxylic acid. The aldehyde's C–H becomes a C–OH. Ketones cannot be oxidized because the ketone carbon has no H to give up. When an exam asks for the product of oxidizing a ketone, write no reaction.
  • Tollens' reagent (silver ion in ammonia solution) oxidizes an aldehyde selectively, even in the presence of other functional groups, and deposits metallic silver as a silver mirror. Ketones: no reaction.
  • Benedict's reagent changes color with compounds that have an aldehyde group or a ketone with an adjacent hydroxyl group (an α-hydroxy ketone): blue Cu2+ → red Cu2O(s). Simple ketones: no reaction. A urine-sugar test is this reaction, because glucose is an aldehyde (Chapter 8).

The two reagents both test for the aldehyde group, but they look different and do not respond to exactly the same compounds. The table summarizes what to expect.

In the Lab

Tollens’ test: aldehydes reduce Ag+ to a silver mirror on the tube; ketones give no reaction. Iodoform test: a methyl ketone (CH3–C=O) reacts with I2 in NaOH to give a yellow solid, iodoform (CHI3), with a medicinal odor (the equation is drawn in the lecture-slide figure in Section 5.1). You will combine these results with the odors from Section 5.2 to identify an unknown carbonyl compound.

Reduction: [H] adds two hydrogens across the C=O

Reduction is the opposite of oxidation. Hydrogen gas with a metal catalyst (Ni, Pd or Pt) or a hydride reagent such as sodium borohydride, NaBH4, adds two H atoms across the C=O: one to the carbon, one to the oxygen. The carbonyl carbon keeps whatever it already had, so the number of carbons attached to it tells you the class of the alcohol.

  • Aldehyde (C=O carbon holds one C and one H) → 1° alcohol.
  • Ketone (C=O carbon holds two C) → 2° alcohol.
  • You can never get a 3° alcohol by reducing a carbonyl, and the reverse is also true: a 3° alcohol has no H on the carbinol carbon, so it cannot be oxidized to a carbonyl (Chapter 4).

Practice (lecture style). An aldehyde is treated with [H]; the same aldehyde is treated with [O]. A ketone is treated with [H]; the same ketone with [O]. Products: aldehyde → 1° alcohol and carboxylic acid; ketone → 2° alcohol and no reaction. The matching-style question on the exam gives you exactly these four options.

Professor Tip

Oxidation and reduction are the same staircase walked in opposite directions: 1° alcohol ↔ aldehyde → carboxylic acid, and 2° alcohol ↔ ketone (stop). Every [O] step adds a C–O bond or removes a C–H; every [H] step does the reverse. If you can name the alcohol you would get back by reduction, you can also name the carbonyl you would get by oxidizing it.

The same redox in the body

Clinical Connection: Nursing

An aldehyde and the hangover. Alcohol dehydrogenase oxidizes ethanol (a 1° alcohol) to ethanal (acetaldehyde), the same alcohol → aldehyde oxidation you learned in Chapter 4, with NAD+ as the oxidizing agent the section describes. Aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH2) then oxidizes the aldehyde to acetate. Acetaldehyde is toxic and reactive; people with a common, less active ALDH2 variant accumulate it and experience facial flushing, a rapid heartbeat and nausea after a small drink, which is also how the disulfiram ("Antabuse") treatment mentioned in the section works. Testing for sugar. Copper-reduction tablets (Clinitest, a Benedict's-type reagent) detect any reducing sugar in urine, including galactose and lactose; glucose meters and dipsticks use the enzyme glucose oxidase and respond to glucose only (Chapter 8).

Summary

  • In organic chemistry, an oxidation increases C–O bonds or decreases C–H bonds; a reduction does the reverse. Write [O] or [H] over the arrow.
  • 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; 2° alcohol → ketone; 3° alcohols and ketones are not oxidized.
  • Reduction adds H across a C=C (hydrogenation, with Ni, Pd or Pt) or a C=O (aldehyde → 1° alcohol, ketone → 2° alcohol).
  • Tollens’ (silver mirror) and Benedict’s (brick-red Cu2O) tests detect aldehydes; ketones give no reaction.
  • In the body, NAD+/NADH carry out these oxidations and reductions, and two thiols are oxidized to a disulfide.
