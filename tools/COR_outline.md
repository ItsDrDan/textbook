# CHEM 3B – Introductory Organic & Biological Chemistry (Fresno City College) – Course Outline of Record
Term effective Spring 2027. 4 units (3 lecture + 1 lab). Prerequisite CHEM 3A. C-ID CHEM 102.
Description: Introduction to the basic concepts of organic and biochemistry. Structure and behavior of organic and biological compounds, metabolism, and regulation. Primarily for students in the health-oriented professions.

## Course objectives
1. Perform structural formula-to-name conversions for organic and biochemical compounds.
2. Describe physical properties of organic compounds.
3. Predict products of representative chemical reactions.
4. Describe the major organic functional groups and identify the major categories of biological compounds.
5. Organize and illustrate the function and processes that enzymes and coenzymes perform in each of the major metabolic pathways.
6. Compare and contrast DNA replication, transcription, RNA translation, and common types of mutation.

## Student learning outcomes
1. Draw and name structures for common mono-functional organic molecules and differentiate functional groups when they appear in organic molecules.
2. Employ the concept of functional groups to predict both chemical and physical properties of an organic molecule.
3. Recognize functional groups in structures of biological molecules and classify biological molecules based on structure.
4. Employ the concept of organic functional groups to predict both chemical and physical properties of biological molecules.
5. Collect, record, organize and analyze experimental data and recognize the limitations of measurements.

Evaluation: ACS-style midterm and final (matching, MC, T/F, fill-in, short answer); "compare and contrast, predict behaviors of organic and biological molecules and/or analyze graphics". Textbook listed: Timberlake GOB (OER may be substituted).

## Lecture content (COR unit → book chapter)
### COR 1 Organic Compounds → Book Ch 1
1.1 Introduction: representations of organic compounds – Lewis structures, condensed structural formulas, expanded structural formulas, line (skeletal) structures.
1.2 Shape and polarity: shape of simple molecules (2–4 electron groups), bond angles, bond polarity, molecular polarity.
1.3 Functional groups.

### COR 2 Alkanes, Alkenes, and Alkynes → Book Ch 2 (alkanes) and Ch 3 (alkenes/alkynes)
2.1 Classification of carbons (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary).
2.2 Saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.
2.3 Isomers and conformers: constitutional (structural) isomers – skeletal, positional; stereoisomers – cis–trans (alkenes; cycloalkanes optional); recognizing single bonds rotate to give conformations; recognizing cycloalkanes are not planar.
2.4 IUPAC nomenclature: unbranched (-ane, -ene, -yne); cyclic (-ane, -ene; mono-, di-, polysubstituted); substituents – halogens, unbranched alkyl.
2.5 Physical properties: IMFs (dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding); ranking molecules by relative melting point, boiling point, solubility.
2.6 Types of reactions: addition, substitution, elimination.
2.7 Reactions of alkanes: combustion, halogenation.
2.8 Reactions of alkenes: addition – hydrogenation, halogenation, hydrohalogenation and hydration (Markovnikov's rule); combustion.
2.9 Polymers.

### COR 3 Aromatic Compounds → Book Ch 3
3.1 Structure of benzene. 3.2 Nomenclature: polysubstituted benzenes incl. ortho/meta/para for disubstituted; benzene ring as a substituent (phenyl).
3.3 Physical properties (IMF ranking: mp, bp, solubility). 3.4 Overview of polynuclear (polycyclic) aromatic hydrocarbons.
3.5 Reactions of benzene: overview of electrophilic aromatic substitution – halogenation, nitration, sulfonation.

### COR 4 Alcohols, Phenols, Thiols, and Ethers → Book Ch 4
4.1 Structure: classification of alcohols (1°, 2°, 3°); isomers – constitutional: skeletal, positional, functional.
4.2 Nomenclature (IUPAC; halogen or unbranched alkyl substituents): alcohols, phenols, ethers, thiols.
4.3 Physical properties: IMF ranking (mp, bp, solubility); acidity – alcohol vs phenol.
4.4 Reactions of alcohols and thiols: dehydration (elimination) of alcohols – Zaitsev's rule, reversibility (Le Chatelier's principle); oxidation of alcohols and thiols.

### COR 5 Aldehydes and Ketones → Book Ch 5
5.1 Structures: carbonyl functional groups. 5.2 Nomenclature (IUPAC; halogen, unbranched alkyl, hydroxy, alkoxy substituents); aldehydes and ketones; priority of functional groups for suffixes.
5.3 Physical properties (IMF ranking). 5.4 Reactions: oxidation and reduction – Tollens' reagent, Benedict's reagent; addition of alcohol to aldehydes/ketones – hemiacetals, acetals, cyclic hemiacetals and acetals; hydrolysis of acetals and hemiacetals.

### COR 6 Chirality → Book Ch 5 (5.5 Introduction to stereochemistry)
6.1 Handedness. 6.2 Identifying and counting chiral centers. 6.3 Stereoisomers: enantiomers, diastereomers. 6.4 Fischer projections: D- and L- notation.

### COR 7 Carboxylic Acids and Esters → Book Ch 6
7.1 Structures: acyl functional groups. 7.2 Nomenclature (IUPAC; halogen, unbranched alkyl, hydroxy, alkoxy, oxo substituents); benzoic acid derivatives.
7.3 Physical properties: IMF ranking; acidity of carboxylic acids – weak acids, solubility of carboxylate salts.
7.4 Reactions of carboxylic acids: acid–base neutralization; condensation – Fischer esterification.
7.5 Reactions of esters: hydrolysis – acid-catalyzed; base-promoted (saponification).

### COR 8 Amines and Amides → Book Ch 7
8.1 Structures: classification of amines and amides (1°, 2°, 3°, quaternary). 8.2 Heterocyclic amines. 8.3 Nomenclature (IUPAC incl. aniline derivatives).
8.4 Physical properties: IMF ranking; basicity of amines and amides – weak bases, solubility of ammonium salts.
8.5 Reactions of amines: acid/base neutralization. 8.6 Preparation of amides: nucleophilic acyl substitution – amidation. 8.7 Reactions of amides: hydrolysis – acid-catalyzed, base-catalyzed.
8.8 Neurotransmitters: definition, structure, general function. 8.9 Recognizing functional groups in macromolecules (polyesters, polyamides, peptides).

### COR 9 Carbohydrates → Book Ch 8
9.1 Classification of monosaccharides: functional group (aldoses, ketoses), number of carbons.
9.2 Structure: Fischer projections; isomerism – functional group isomers; chirality – enantiomers (D/L), diastereomers – epimers; cyclic forms – hemiacetals: α and β anomers, Haworth projections, mutarotation.
9.3 Physical properties. 9.4 Common monosaccharides: glucose, galactose, fructose.
9.5 Oxidation and reduction of monosaccharides: oxidation to acidic sugars – aldonic, aldaric, alduronic acids; reducing sugars; reduction to sugar alcohols.
9.6 Disaccharides and polysaccharides: maltose, lactose, sucrose, starch (amylose, amylopectin), glycogen, cellulose; polymerization – condensation (glycosidic linkages); hydrolysis of glycosidic linkages.

### COR 10 Lipids → Book Ch 9
10.1 Properties of lipids. 10.2 Non-hydrolyzable lipids: steroids – cholesterol, bile salts, steroid hormones (sex hormones, corticosteroids); fat-soluble vitamins; eicosanoids – prostaglandins, leukotrienes, thromboxanes.
10.3 Hydrolyzable lipids: fatty acids – saturation, cis–trans isomers, melting point, omega terminology; waxes; triacylglycerols – fat vs oil, hydrogenation, overview of oxidation, hydrolysis/saponification.
10.4 Storage and membrane lipids: glycerolipids, phospholipids, sphingolipids; cell membranes – diffusion, facilitated, and active transport.

### COR 11 Amino Acids and Proteins → Book Ch 10
11.1 Structure of amino acids: classification of R groups; 1- and 3-letter abbreviations; stereochemistry – D/L notation.
11.2 Amino acids as acids and bases: zwitterion; isoelectric point – structure above, below, and at pI.
11.3 Peptides: polymerization – condensation (peptide linkage), hydrolysis of peptide linkage; naming peptides.
11.4 Protein structure: primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary. 11.5 Protein hydrolysis and denaturation.

### COR 12 Enzymes and Vitamins → Book Ch 11
12.1 Structure and function: review of reaction rate and catalysts; specificity; models of enzyme action.
12.2 Classification: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases.
12.3 Activity and regulation: effect of pH, temperature, concentration; allosteric control, feedback control, covalent modification; enzyme inhibition; cofactors and vitamins.

### COR 13 Nucleic Acids and Protein Synthesis → Book Ch 12
13.1 Components. 13.2 Structure: primary, secondary; DNA vs RNA; types of RNA. 13.3 DNA replication. 13.4 Transcription and translation – genetic code.
13.5 Genetic mutations. 13.6 Overview of recombinant DNA. 13.7 Overview of viruses.

### COR 14 Metabolic Pathways for Carbohydrates → Book Ch 13
14.1 Overview of metabolism stages and energy: catabolism and anabolism; important coenzymes.
14.2 Digestion of carbohydrates. 14.3 Glycolysis – emphasis on identifying reaction types, enzyme classification, coenzymes; entry of other monosaccharides; regulation.
14.4 Pathways of pyruvate under aerobic and anaerobic conditions. 14.5 Pentose phosphate pathway.
14.6 Glycogen synthesis and degradation: glycogenesis (reaction types, enzyme classification); glycogenolysis (contrast with glycogenesis); regulation.
14.7 Gluconeogenesis: contrast with glycolysis; Cori cycle; regulation.

### COR 15 Metabolism and Energy → Book Ch 14
15.1 Citric acid cycle: identify reaction types, enzyme classification, coenzymes; carbon balance; reactions with energy input/output; regulation.
15.2 Electron transport: chain – complexes I–IV, electron carriers; oxidative phosphorylation; regulation.
15.3 Calculating net ATP from glucose: from glycolysis (including the malate–aspartate shuttle), from the citric acid cycle, from degradation of pyruvate.

### COR 16 Metabolic Pathways for Lipids → Book Ch 15
16.1 Digestion of lipids: triacylglycerols; fat stores and mobilization. 16.2 Overview of glycerol metabolism.
16.3 Oxidation of fatty acids: activation, transport (carnitine shuttle), β-oxidation cycle (reaction types, enzyme classes, coenzymes), oxidation of unsaturated fatty acids, net ATP from fatty acid oxidation.
16.4 Ketone bodies: overview of ketogenesis. 16.5 Fatty acid synthesis (lipogenesis) – contrast with β-oxidation.

### COR 17 Metabolic Pathways for Amino Acids → Book Ch 15
17.1 Digestion of proteins. 17.2 Amino acid pool and protein turnover; positive and negative nitrogen balance.
17.3 Degradation: transamination, oxidative deamination, urea cycle (reaction types, enzyme classification), carbon skeleton degradation.
17.4 Amino acid synthesis: essential vs non-essential. 17.5 Overlap of molecules between covered metabolic processes.

## Lab content (for "In the Lab" boxes only – do not write lab procedures)
Hydrocarbons (properties; tests distinguishing alkanes/alkenes); alcohols & phenols (tests; unknown ID); aldehydes & ketones (tests; unknown ID); carboxylic acids & esters (neutralization; esterification/hydrolysis); amines & amides (ammonium salt solubility; neutralization; amidation/hydrolysis); synthesis of aspirin or acetaminophen; carbohydrates (distinguishing sugars; unknown sugar; hydrolysis); lipids (saponification; soap properties); amino acids & proteins (chromatography; denaturation of albumin; isolation of casein; amino acid vs protein tests); DNA extraction; enzymes (effects of concentration, temperature, pH, inhibitor); vitamins (water vs fat soluble; ascorbic acid titration); metabolism (digestion of foods; analysis of "urine" – glucose, ketone bodies, protein, urobilinogen, electrolytes).
