## What to do, per chapter
1. **Terminology and voice.** Where the slides use a specific phrase, rule, or mnemonic ("count the carbons on the OH carbon", "LEO goes GER", "the more substituted alkene wins", a naming checklist, the way he writes 1°/2°/3°), use the same wording in the Chapter Guide's Key Ideas, Professor Tips and worked examples. Keep "Professor Tip" as the box title. Do not import slide text verbatim as prose paragraphs; match vocabulary and the order of ideas, not sentences.
2. **Order of topics.** If the lecture presents topics in a different order than the guide's Key Ideas, note it; reorder *content inside sections* where it helps, but **do not rename or reorder existing `<h2>`/`<h3>` headings** (the build anchors on them). If a reorder would really help, say so in the report instead.
3. **Assessment alignment.** Make a checklist of every skill named in the relevant exam review / study guide / sample exam for your chapters. For each: (a) is it taught in the Chapter Guide with a worked example? (b) does the Practice Problems page have at least two problems exercising it (in a comparable format: MC, fill-in, matching, short answer, "draw the structure")? Fill gaps with new, original worked examples and problems (continue numbering; keep every answer reasoned). Mirror the *formats* he uses (matching, fill-in, T/F with correction, predict-the-product, name-from-structure) without reusing content.
4. **Scope guard.** Anything a majors-level organic or biochemistry course would teach but the slides, exam reviews and COR never do (reaction mechanisms with arrows, hybridization beyond what the slides mention, R/S, Newman projections, enzyme kinetics equations, detailed pharmacology) must go. If the slides touch a topic lightly (e.g., "n-, sec-, tert-, iso-" prefixes, hybridization words), the book may mention it at that same light level.
5. **Slide-figure descriptions.** The Slide*.png figures (in `figures/`) have one-sentence alt text. Open each PNG used in your chapters with the Read tool and write a fuller `alt` (2–4 sentences: what is drawn, left to right / top to bottom, with the names and the key labels), so a screen-reader user gets the same information a sighted student does. Keep the `<figcaption>` short.
6. **Consistency pass** with the rest of the book: names of games, box titles, "Figure N.0:" captions, Problem/Solution/Check examples, 1&deg; notation.
7. Run `python3 tools/check_fragment.py <file> --guide|--problems|--section` on every file you touch, and preview anything whose layout you changed.
