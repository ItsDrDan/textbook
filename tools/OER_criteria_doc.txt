OER Criteria Analysis for the CHEM 3B LibreTexts Textbook
 · 
Summary
To meet the SCCCD/California community college bar, the CHEM 3B book must be (1) legally open with clean TASL attribution and a compatible license, (2) accessible to WCAG 2.1 AA, (3) aligned to the CHEM 3B Course Outline of Record, and (4) free to students so sections can carry the ZTC schedule label.
Two SCCCD Canvas shells were reviewed:
Open Educational Resources (course 33246): FCC's OER resource hub, run by OER Liaison Sally Potter. It has no Modules — 14 Pages plus a Files area of flex-day slide decks. It defines OER vs. ZTC, lists the six CC licenses, and gives a 5-step remix workflow.
CELA-308 \"OER 101\" (course 108299, Spring 2024): Reedley College's 6-module OER certificate course taught by OER Librarian Sarah Micu Kershaw. This is where the actual standards live: licensing, attribution (TASL), license compatibility, adaptation workflow, accessibility, UDL, and open pedagogy.
Neither course contains a formal scored rubric for textbooks. The criteria below combine what the courses teach with the outside standards they point to (COOL4Ed accessibility review, BCcampus Accessibility Toolkit, Section 508/WCAG, CA Ed Code 66406.9).
Top priorities for the book, in order:
A front-matter Licensing & Attribution page naming the book's license (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 if Ball et al. stays the core source), the Ball et al. source, and every other remixed source with TASL.
Per-page or per-chapter attributions stating \"Adapted from…\" plus a log of what was changed.
License audit of every image, game widget and figure: no ND, no all-rights-reserved, no unverified Wikimedia/Google images.
Accessibility pass on every page: real headings, alt text on every structure/figure, captions, color-independent meaning, accessible embedded games, accessible math/chem notation.
COR alignment map: each chapter tied to CHEM 3B SLOs/objectives (backward design).
Zero cost to students: free online, low-cost print optional, so sections qualify for the ZTC icon.

Licensing and attribution
The book's license is set by its most restrictive ShareAlike source: Ball et al. is CC BY-NC-SA, so the remix must be CC BY-NC-SA too (the same or a later version; check the exact version on the LibreTexts source pages).

This matters for selling games on Teachers Pay Teachers: anything derived from NC book content can't be sold. Games and widgets Dan writes from scratch remain his own and can carry a separate license.
Attribution rules (TASL)
Every reused item gets Title, Author, Source link, License link. Example: \"Alkanes\" by David W. Ball et al., from The Basics of GOB Chemistry, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0.
Modified material says \"Adapted from…\" and briefly describes the changes.
Text attributions go at the foot of each page or at the chapter end. Image attributions go in the caption or directly under the figure.
Keep the original creator's copyright notice intact, and don't imply the original authors endorse the remix.
Use the Open Attribution Builder (WA SBCTC) for consistent wording.
Required notices in the book
Licensing page (front matter): book license with deed link; \"Remixed and adapted by [author/FCC Chemistry] from…\" listing every source text; statement that third-party items keep their own licenses.
Change log / adaptation statement: what was reordered, rewritten, added (Professor Tips, health-science examples, games) and removed, per the course's \"keep a list of changes\" guidance. Minor typo fixes don't need logging.
Original content statement: new sections, figures and games written by the author, with the license applied to them.
SCCCD intellectual property check: confirm under the faculty contract and AR that the author can openly license work made for the course.
Images and media
Use only CC, CC0 or public-domain images, with verified provenance; the course warns that Wikimedia uploads are sometimes mislabeled.
Structures drawn by Dan's own game engine are original work; label them as such in the figure credits.
Videos embedded or linked must be CC-licensed or simply linked, and must have accurate captions.
Accessibility
The legal target is WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Under DOJ's ADA Title II rule, as extended by an interim final rule on April 20, 2026, SCCCD must comply by April 26, 2027 if it counts as a 50,000+ population entity (April 26, 2028 if smaller). LMS content is explicitly covered, and Ed Code 66406.9 requires ZTC-labeled materials to comply with the ADA.

Quality, alignment and pedagogy
The courses judge OER quality by alignment to the Course Outline of Record, accuracy, currency, cultural relevance and UDL. Neither shell gives a scored rubric, so the criteria below come from what they teach.
COR alignment (backward design). Pull the CHEM 3B COR from eLumen (FCC public site). Map every course objective and SLO to a chapter or section, and show it in each chapter's learning objectives. Flag any objective with no content, and any content beyond the COR (the \"no chemistry-majors material\" rule).
Learning objectives at the top of each chapter, phrased as measurable student actions that match the COR verbs.
Accuracy and currency. Correct errors from the source, update clinical and nutrition data, and keep conventions consistent (enzyme classes, inhibitor types, essential amino acids). Ball et al. dates from about 2011, so review drug, diet and disease examples for currency.
Cultural relevance and equity. Examples should fit FCC/Central Valley health-science students: nursing, dental hygiene, radiation technology, respiratory therapy, agriculture, and local health issues such as diabetes and valley fever. Use inclusive names and images, and avoid stereotypes.
UDL: multiple means of representation, action and engagement. Text plus structures plus video plus interactive games, with practice in several formats and an answer key or immediate feedback.
Ancillaries that link back to the text. End-of-chapter practice, Practice Games, and possibly H5P or ADAPT question banks, all with matching attributions.
Open pedagogy (optional, valued). Renewable assignments where students write practice problems or clinical connection boxes that feed back into the book, licensed with student consent.
Readability and consistency. Glossary of key terms, consistent figure styles, and a uniform Professor Tip / Clinical Connection format.
Peer review. Have a colleague (e.g., another CHEM 3B instructor) and the FCC OER Liaison review it before adoption; record the review in the front matter.
Living document. Version number, date and errata or feedback contact.
ZTC labeling and California requirements
Sections that use only this book (plus other free materials) can carry the ZTC icon in the schedule. California Ed Code 66406.9, operative July 1, 2024, requires community colleges to highlight such courses.
