## 4.5: Reactions of Alcohols (page 597094)

**4.5 In the Lab: chromate test kept as chemistry but no longer called part of the lab; Experiment 4 Lucas test added**  
Why: exp04.js has no chromate/dichromate test; Part C is the Lucas test (ZnCl2 in conc. HCl; R-OH -> R-Cl, cloudy; 3 deg < 1 min, 2 deg 3-10 min or warm 50-60 C bath, 1 deg no change; phenols do not react).

- Before:

> In the Lab
> 
> Chromate oxidation test (alcohols and phenols experiment): orange chromium(VI) (dichromate, Cr2O72−, in the acidic reagent) turns green (Cr3+) when it oxidizes a 1° or 2° alcohol. A 3° alcohol cannot be oxidized, so the solution stays orange. The balanced equation in the section text is this same color change written out: dichromate is the orange reactant, Cr3+ the green product.

- After:

> In the Lab
> 
> Chromate oxidation test (Figure 4.5.3): orange chromium(VI) (dichromate, Cr2O72−, in the acidic reagent) turns green (Cr3+) when it oxidizes a 1° or 2° alcohol. A 3° alcohol cannot be oxidized, so the solution stays orange. The balanced equation in the section text is this same color change written out: dichromate is the orange reactant, Cr3+ the green product.
> 
> Experiment 4 – Alcohols and Phenols does not use the chromate test. It sorts alcohols with the Lucas test instead: zinc chloride in concentrated HCl replaces the –OH with –Cl, and the alkyl chloride that forms does not dissolve, so the tube turns cloudy. A 3° alcohol turns cloudy almost at once, a 2° alcohol in about 3–10 minutes (some need a warm water bath), and a 1° alcohol stays clear. This is a substitution, not an oxidation, so the order is reversed: the 3° alcohol that chromate cannot oxidize reacts fastest with the Lucas reagent.
