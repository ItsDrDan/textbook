# Instructional-quality review, Chapters 6–10 (2026-11-13)

Read-only review; no repo file was edited. Scores are 0–4. Columns: Acc = scientific accuracy, Clar = conceptual clarity, Prereq = prerequisite accessibility, Vis = visual quality (from alt text, captions and placement only; no image was viewed), Reas = reasoning transparency, Prac = practice alignment, Trans = transfer. "n/a" or "n/j" = not applicable or not judged (Practice Problems pages were checked for sequencing and level mix only).

How it was done: I read Chapters 6 and 8 myself. Chapters 7, 9 and 10 were each read by a separate reviewer agent working to the same brief; I read their reports, spot-checked the main claims against the page text (7.7 Solution b, the 10.2 pI rule, the 10.10 temperatures, the Chapter 7 readiness link), corrected one proposed edit (7.7), and re-ran the uniqueness check on every `old` string. Scores for 7, 9 and 10 are theirs, so calibration between chapters may differ by about half a point.

## The pattern across all five chapters

1. **The inherited half and the Lecture Connection teach the same thing twice, and sometimes disagree.** Examples: 6.8 "catalyzed by … a base" vs "the base is a reactant"; 7.6 "slow amide at room temperature" vs "only the salt forms"; 7.1 and 6.1 common names first vs "use IUPAC on exams"; 9.5 "highly polar" lipids; 10.2 the above-pI rule vs "zwitterion at pH 7.4".
2. **Inherited worked examples state the answer; the author's own examples explain and check.** The weak ones are 6.1, 6.4, 6.8, 7.1, 7.7, 8.4 Example 1 and 10.5 Example 1.
3. **Practice is uneven by page.** No worked example, exercise or Try It on 6.6, 8.3 (no exercise or Try It), 9.4, 10.7 and 10.9; no on-page practice on 7.5, 7.9, 7.10 and 10.3. The Chapter Guides and Practice Problems cover most of these, so this is a placement gap, not a content gap.
4. **Inherited exercises with no answer on the page.** 6.1, 6.4, 6.5 and 6.8 have eight inherited exercise boxes between them and none shows an answer (the only answers on those pages belong to the Valley and Molecule in the Clinic questions); the Chapter 7 reviewer reports the same for 7.1 Exercises 1–6. (REVIEW lists pages whose answers do not print; these have none at all.)
5. **Clinical boxes are strong on content but most ask nothing.** The Valley boxes with a "Think about it" (6.5, 8.6, 10.10, 7.3) are the ones that make the student use the chemistry; 9.2's can be answered from 9.1 without the box, and 7.11 and 10.9 have no question.
6. **Terms used before they are taught:** "isotonic" (8.3, taught 9.5), "α1–β2 link" (8.5, taught 8.6), "active site/substrate" (8.6, taught Ch 11), "pI" (10.1, taught 10.2), "lipid" (9.1 first sentence), "nucleophilic acyl substitution" (7.6, never taught), "σ-bonds" (10.6), "steric strain" (10.7).

### Chapter 6 notes (read by me)

- **6.1** Example 1 asks for "the common and IUPAC names" and its solution leads with γ-chlorobutyric acid; the page then says "On exams, use the IUPAC name". Example 2 is "β-chloropropionic acid". A student who works the examples practises the form they are told not to write.
- **6.3** The Professor Tip's odor explanation ("too long to dissolve in the water of saliva") is offered as a prediction but is not something the section's rules let a student derive.
- **6.4, 6.8** Clinical boxes (citrate tubes, enamel, aspirin, benzocaine) carry real chemistry but none ends in a question; the same content is asked well in 6.12 (old aspirin, two enamel patients).
- **6.7** The page's only worked esterification is in the 11-minute video. The Professor Tip describes the erase-and-join method in words; the Guide (Example 2) is where it is first worked in print.
- **6.9** Two Professor Tips in a row; the first says "only recognize", yet nothing on the page asks the student to recognize one.
- **6.10** The page has three kinds of content (inherited conjugate-pair theory, the ranking table, the pH 7.4 rule) and no Problem → Solution → Check for any of them. The rule is practised in the Try It and in 6.12, so the fix is a pointer or moving one Guide-style example, not new text.
- **6.11** "Acid hydrolysis: ester + H2O (H+) → acid + alcohol" in Key Idea 3 uses a one-way arrow; Key Idea 7 says "reversible". Small, not in REVIEW.

### Chapter 8 notes (read by me)

- **8.2** "Diastereomers" and "epimers" lean on Chapter 5 vocabulary; the table handles it well. The source "Note" ("another type of stereoisomers than the cis-trans … previously discussed") is awkward English.
