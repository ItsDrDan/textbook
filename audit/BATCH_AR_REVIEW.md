## 15.5: Ketone Bodies and Ketoacidosis (page 597213)

**Ketone figures re-sourced: blood 0.1-0.25 mmol/L (~1-3 mg/100 mL); "20 mg/day" removed**  
Why: Puchalska & Crawford, Cell Metab 2017;25:262 (PMC5313038): total ketone bodies in healthy adults "normally exhibit circadian oscillations between approximately 100-250 uM", rising to ~1 mM after 24 h fasting; "A very small fraction of liver-derived ketone bodies can be readily measured in the urine." Conversion: 0.1-0.25 mmol/L x ~103 g/mol = 10-26 mg/L = about 1-3 mg/100 mL, so the old "about 1 mg/100 mL" sits at the low end. No legitimate source found for 20 mg/day (NCBI Bookshelf and StatPearls blocked automated access; a 1958 field study, seen only as a secondary copy, implies much more than 20 mg/day), so the number was removed rather than kept unsourced.

- Before:

> Under normal conditions, the kidneys excrete about 20 mg of ketone bodies each day, and the blood levels are maintained at about 1 mg of ketone bodies per 100 mL of blood.

- After:

> Under normal conditions, blood ketone bodies stay low, about 0.1 to 0.25 mmol/L (roughly 1 to 3 mg per 100 mL of blood), and only a very small amount is lost in the urine (Source: Puchalska P, Crawford PA, Multi-dimensional roles of ketone bodies in fuel metabolism, signaling, and therapeutics [→ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313038/], Cell Metabolism, 2017).
