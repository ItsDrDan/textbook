Top P1 items:
1. **597192 (13.4), "To Your Health: Diabetes":** "Current CDC estimates are about 38 million Americans with diabetes" is out of date. The CDC National Diabetes Statistics Report now gives **40.1 million (12.0%, 2023 data)**, and the CDC "About Diabetes" page says "More than 40 million Americans." The sentence also has no citation.
2. **597192 (13.4):** "It increases the risk of having a heart attack or stroke by two to four times." CDC now says people with diabetes have "twice the risk for heart disease." The 2–4× figure is an older AHA mortality figure, and the sentence has no source.

The Rosa's Story check passed. Every clinical number in Parts 13, 14 and 15 is plausible and agrees with current CDC, ADA and MSD guidance:
- Part 13: glucose 62 mg/dL (below 70 counts as hypoglycemia). She is treated with 15 g of carbohydrate as four tablets, then rechecked after 15 minutes at 104 mg/dL.
- Part 14: a 30-minute walk burns about 140 kcal (CDC figure), and the text names the risk factors for metformin lactic acidosis.
- Part 15: moderate urine ketones after two days without eating, with a normal blood pH, is starvation ketosis. Later lab work adds BUN and creatinine, and her A1c is 7.0%.

Two small gaps remain (Table B). Part 15 says "treating gum disease can modestly improve blood glucose control" with no source (Cochrane 2022: about 0.4% lower HbA1c). Part 13 says "four glucose tablets" equals 15 g, while CDC says 3–4 tablets. That is consistent and not an error.
