## 6. Recommendation: a "Sources for data" note

**Recommendation: one short note in the front matter, plus in-line citations only in the three new labs.** Experiments 1–8 contain standard textbook chemistry, GHS hazard statements, and physical constants that all come from a few standard references. One front-matter note covers them without cluttering each lab, and it matches the house style, which has no footnotes in the procedures. The urinalysis, digestion, and soap labs make clinical and food-composition claims (reference ranges, renal values, fat composition). Those should carry a short "Sources" line at the end of the Introduction, in the same style as the textbook's source lists.

Suggested placement: after "Before Every Lab" in frontmatter.js (an h3 "Sources for Data"), or at the end of Appendix F.

Draft text (front matter):

> **Sources for Data**
> Hazard statements in this manual follow the Globally Harmonized System (GHS) classifications for each chemical as summarized by PubChem (National Library of Medicine, https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) from the European Chemicals Agency C&L Inventory, and the supplier's Safety Data Sheet (SDS). They describe the pure chemical unless a concentration is given. Always read the SDS for the solution you are using. Physical constants (melting point, boiling point, density, solubility) are from PubChem and the NIST Chemistry WebBook (https://webbook.nist.gov), which agree with the *CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics*. Atomic masses are IUPAC standard atomic weights (Appendix F). Clinical reference values (blood glucose, urine tests) are from MedlinePlus (U.S. National Library of Medicine, https://medlineplus.gov) and are typical ranges; each clinical laboratory sets its own.
