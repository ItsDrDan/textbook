## 15.5 Ketone Bodies and Ketoacidosis: Hands-on Lab: Buffers, blood gases and DKA

> Add acid to plain water and to a blood-like buffer of H2CO3 and HCO3−, and watch which pH holds. Then work patient cases: a patient in DKA breathing deep and fast, arterial blood gases, lactic acid after a sprint. They are the chemistry behind Kussmaul breathing: breathing out CO2 removes acid. Proton Swap! is a CHEM 3A game; the Buffer Lab is the part this section needs.

## 6.10 Acid-base reactions: Hands-on Lab: Lactic acid and the blood buffer

> The blood buffer, CO2 + H2O ⇌ H+ + HCO3−, runs here as moving particles. Add lactic acid (more H+), hold your breath, breathe fast, or give bicarbonate, and watch the equilibrium shift and the pH tube change color: the Clinical Connection above, one stress at a time. Equilibrium Park! is a CHEM 3A game; this lab is the part that fits this section.

## 10.9 Quaternary structure of proteins: Hands-on Lab: Hemoglobin and oxygen

> Hb + O2 ⇌ HbO2 runs here as moving particles. Climb to high altitude, put on an oxygen mask, or lower the hemoglobin (anemia), and watch how much oxygen the blood carries. The lab counts each oxygen-binding site as one Hb; real hemoglobin has four subunits, and each one binds one O2 at its heme. Equilibrium Park! is a CHEM 3A game; this lab is the part that fits this section.

## 1.5 Characteristics of Molecules: Hands-on Lab: 3-D shapes and polarity

> The organic molecules here come from this course. Use the completed Lewis structure to name the shape around each atom, then turn the 3-D model to check it. When the shapes are easy, switch to the Polarity Lab, which asks this section’s two questions in order: are the bonds polar, and do they cancel?

## 1.3 Drawing Lewis Structures: Hands-on Lab: Lewis Structure Builder

> Build the Lewis structure of an organic molecule from this course, one bond and one lone pair at a time, then check it. Use the same count-first routine as the worked examples: valence electrons, skeleton, bonds, then lone pairs.
