Lecture Connection

Primary structure is the sequence, written N-terminus to C-terminus, and it is the only level held by covalent peptide bonds. One wrong residue can change everything downstream, which is why the lecture spends time on sickle cell hemoglobin and on insulin.

Why one residue matters

  • Primary structure is covalent: it survives heat, alcohol, acid and heavy metals, and only hydrolysis (Section 10.10) breaks it.
  • Sickle cell hemoglobin differs from normal hemoglobin at one position out of 146 in the β chain: Glu → Val at position 6. A charged, hydrophilic side chain is swapped for a hydrophobic one on the surface of the protein. Deoxygenated hemoglobin molecules stick to one another through that hydrophobic patch, form fibers, and bend red cells into sickles that block capillaries. Because the Glu is gone, HbS carries one less negative charge than HbA and moves more slowly toward the positive electrode in electrophoresis (Section 10.2), which is how the lab tells them apart.
  • Insulin is a 51-residue peptide hormone: an A chain (21 residues) and a B chain (30) linked by two disulfide bonds, with a third inside the A chain. Swallowed insulin is just protein to the stomach; pepsin and trypsin hydrolyze it to amino acids, so it must be injected.
  • Aspartame, the sweetener in sugar-free gum, is the methyl ester of the dipeptide Asp-Phe. It is hydrolyzed in the gut to aspartate, phenylalanine and methanol, which is why products that contain it carry a warning for people with phenylketonuria (PKU).

In this course

The section text uses "more than 50 amino acids" as the line between a polypeptide and a protein. Use the lecture cut-offs on exams: oligopeptide 2–20, polypeptide 21–40, protein more than 40 (see the note in Section 10.4). Everything else in this section, including commas versus dashes and the -yl naming rule, matches the lecture exactly.

Worked example

Example 1: Name a tripeptide and find its hydrolysis products

Problem. A tripeptide has the sequence Ala-Cys-Gly. (a) Which residue is the N-terminus and which is the C-terminus? (b) Give its full name and its one-letter form. (c) What products form when it is completely hydrolyzed?

Solution.

(a) Peptides are written from the N-terminus, so alanine (free –NH3+) is the N-terminus and glycine (free –COO−) is the C-terminus. Cysteine in the middle has used both of its groups in the two peptide bonds.

(b) Change the ending of every residue except the last to -yl: alanine → alanyl, cysteine → cysteinyl, glycine stays glycine. Run them together: alanylcysteinylglycine. One-letter form: A, C, G → ACG.

(c) Complete hydrolysis adds one water across each of the two peptide bonds and returns the three free amino acids as zwitterions: alanine + cysteine + glycine. No new compounds form; the residues simply get their –NH3+ and –COO− groups back.

Check. Three residues, two peptide bonds, two waters consumed in hydrolysis. Reversing the letters (GCA, glycylcysteinylalanine) would be a different tripeptide with glycine at the N-terminus, so the order in the name must match the order in the sequence.

Clinical Connection: Nursing

Albumin, the most abundant globular protein in plasma (normal about 3.5–5.0 g/dL), is too large to leave the capillaries, so it holds water in the blood by oncotic pressure (the osmotic pull of plasma proteins). In liver disease, malnutrition or kidney loss, hypoalbuminemia (low albumin) lets fluid leak into tissues: edema. The urine dipstick protein pad detects mainly albumin; a positive result, proteinuria, means the kidney filter is leaking protein that should have stayed in the blood. In phenylketonuria (PKU) the enzyme that converts phenylalanine to tyrosine is missing, phenylalanine builds up and damages the developing brain, so newborns are screened and the diet (and aspartame) is restricted. A single-residue change is enough for disease: in sickle cell disease the Glu → Val swap described above causes vaso-occlusive pain crises and hemolytic anemia. Chapter 15 picks up nitrogen balance: protein intake versus the nitrogen lost as urea, negative after surgery, burns or starvation.
