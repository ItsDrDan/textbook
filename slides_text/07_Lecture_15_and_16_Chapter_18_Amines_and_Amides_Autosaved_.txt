--- slide 22 ---
Physical Properties of Amines
Only 1o and 2o amines can accept and donate hydrogen bonds.
Nitrogen is not as electronegative as oxygen  weaker H-bonds than O-H
3o amines can only accept hydrogen bonds, they can not donate.
Hydrogen bonding is an attractive force between molecules (intermolecular)

--- slide 23 ---
Physical Properties of Amines
Do 1o and 2o amines have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar alcohols? 
Lower! Oxygen is more electronegative and so the hydrogen-bonds between alcohol molecules is stronger.
Do 1o and 2o amines have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar ethers? 
Higher! Ethers can’t form hydrogen bonds.
Do 1o and 2o amines have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar 3o amines? 
Higher! 1o and 2o  amines can form hydrogen bonds and 3o amines can’t.

--- slide 24 ---
Physical Properties of Amines
Methyl-amines and ethyl-amines are gases at room temperature
Most other amines are liquids at room temperature

--- slide 25 ---
Solubility
Can 1o and 2o amines molecules hydrogen bond with water molecules? Would they be soluble in water?
Yes, they can form hydrogen bonds with water. They can accept a hydrogen (proton) from water, and also donate a hydrogen to water
Can 3o amines molecules hydrogen bond with water molecules? Would they be soluble in water?
Yes, they can form hydrogen bonds with water - the lone pair on the N can accept a hydrogen from water!
Not as soluble as 1o or 2o amines because they can not donate a hydrogen
Amines up to about 6 carbon atoms have appreciable solubility in water. Beyond that, the carbon chain is too bulky and limits the molecule’s ability to form hydrogen bonds.

--- slide 52 ---
Physical Properties of Amides
Do hydrogen bond intermolecular forces exist between two amide molecules?
Yes – for primary and secondary amides
No for tertiary amides (No H is covalently bonded to N, O, or F)
Do dipole-dipole intermolecular forces exist between two amide molecules?
Yes! The C=O and C-N bonds are polar (C-N is less polar, but still polar)
Do dispersion intermolecular forces exist between two amide molecules?
Yes! These exist between all molecules

--- slide 53 ---
Physical Properties of Amides: Boiling Points
Do primary and secondary amides have higher or lower melting points/boiling points than similar tertiary amides?
Higher (because primary and secondary amides can form hydrogen bonds with other amides) 
Do 1o and 2o amides have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar esters?
Higher because they can form hydrogen bonds – esters can’t
Do 1o amides have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar 2o amides?
Primary amides have a higher melting point and boiling point because there are more H’s bonded to N to form hydrogen bonds.
Do 3o amides have higher or lower melting points and boiling points than similar alkanes? 
Tertiary amines have higher boiling points than alkanes because they are polar and have dipole-dipole intermolecular forces.

--- slide 54 ---
Physical Properties of Amides: Solubility
Can 1o and 2o amides molecules hydrogen bond with water molecules?
Yes! (They are both proton acceptors and proton donors)
Can 3o amides molecules hydrogen bond with water molecules?
Yes! (they can be proton-acceptors only)
Are 1o and 2o amides soluble in water?
Yes! (for 5 carbons or less)
Are 3o amides soluble in water?
Yes! (not as much as 10 or 20 though because they can only accept protons)

Neurotransmitters

--- slide 67 ---
Bio: Neurotransmitters
A neurotransmitter is a chemical messenger that transmits nerve impulses from one neuron to another.
The space between the two neurons is called a synapse.
The presynaptic neuron releases the neurotransmitter.
The postsynaptic neuron contains the receptors that bind the neurotransmitter

--- slide 68 ---
Bio: Neurotransmitters
Neurotransmitters:
are substances present in the human body that assist in passing nerve impulses from one cell to another.
contain N atoms as amines and alkylammonium ions.
are synthesized from compounds such as amino acids obtained from our diets.
their amino groups are usually ionized, forming ammonium cations and carboxylate anions.
[notes] When norepinephrine levels increase, a person feels elated, and then manic if levels go too high.
Norepinephrine is converted into adrenaline when a person experiences fear or stress.

Dopamine affects the brain processes that control movement, emotions, and pleasure.
Patients with Parkinson’s disease have decreased levels of dopamine in the body

Final TakeAways
Amines and amides are found in many biological molecules including proteins and neurotransmitters
Amines are bases
Amines and Amides can be classified as primary, secondary or tertiary.
Ammonium salts are more soluble in water 
Amides can be made from carboxylic acids and amines
Same nucleophilic acyl substitution as esters and carboxylic acids.
Physical properties such as boiling and solubility can be explained in terms of intermolecular forces.
