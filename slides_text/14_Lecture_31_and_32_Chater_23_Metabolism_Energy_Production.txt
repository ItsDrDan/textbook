--- slide 6 ---
Metabolism
Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions that take place in an organism – providing energy for the body
Catabolism is the breakdown of large molecules into smaller ones
Energy is generally released during catabolism
Anabolism is the synthesis of large molecules from smaller ones
Energy is generally absorbed during anabolism
[notes] Energy released = catabolic
Energy absorbed = anabolic

--- slide 7 ---
Metabolic Pathways
Linear Metabolic Pathway 
A series of reactions that generates a final product. 
Product is different from any of the reactants.
Cyclic Pathway
A series of reactions that regenerates the first reactant.
[notes] Often, the process is a series of consecutive reactions called a metabolic pathway, which can be linear or cyclic.

Stages of Metabolism
Catabolic reactions are organized as: 
Stage 1: 	Digestion and hydrolysis break down
			       large molecules to smaller ones that
			       enter the bloodstream
Stage 2: 	Degradation breaks down molecules to
			       two- and three-carbon compounds

                      citric acid cycle and electron transport

Citric Acid Cycle - Overview
The main function of the citric acid cycle is to produce reduced coenzymes (NADH and FADH2).
These molecules enter the electron transport chain and ultimately produce ATP.

--- slide 26 ---
Citric Acid Cycle - Overview
The overall citric acid cycle yields:
2		CO2 molecules
3		NADH and 	1	FADH2 molecules
1		GTP molecule
The “fuel” for the cycle is acetyl CoA, obtained from the breakdown of carbohydrates, fats, and proteins.

--- slide 27 ---
Regulation of Citric Acid Cycle
The reaction rate for the citric acid cycle is controlled by the body’s need for ATP (in step 1):
when ATP levels are high, ATP inhibits citrate synthase when ATP levels are low, ADP activates citrate synthase
Similarly, ADP and NADH controls isocitrate dehydrogenase in step 3: 
NADH acts as an inhibitor 
ADP acts as an activator

Malate-Aspartate Shuttle
The NADH produced in step 6 of glycolysis is produced in the cytosol.
The electron transport chain occurs in the mitochondrion.

Malate-Aspartate Shuttle
The malate–aspartate shuttle transfers the hydrogen ions and electrons from the cytosol to the mitochondrion:
In the cytosol, oxaloacetate is reduced with NADH and the malate dehydrogenase.
The electrons and hydrogen ions are carried across the membrane by a malate transporter. 
In the mitochondrion, malate is oxidized back to oxaloacetate. NAD+ is reduced to NADH.
The electrons from NADH can now enter the electron transport chain.

Electron Transport Chain
The electron transport chain is a multistep process using 4 enzyme complexes (I, II, III and IV) located along the mitochondrial inner membrane.
Transfers hydrogen ions and electrons from NADH and FADH2 until they react with oxygen to H2O.
[notes] Strips H+ ions off coenzymes, electrons held by metal ions temporarily.I) NADH to NAD2) FADH2 to FAD
3) Brings e- to IV
4) Combine to form water

--- slide 36 ---
Electron Transport Chain: Electron Carriers
The electron transport chain has two electron carriers, coenzyme Q and cytochrome c, attached to the mitochondrial inner membrane, that carry electrons between the enzyme complexes (I, II, III and IV) .
CoQ, is 	fat		soluble and can move laterally within the mitochondrial membrane.
The Q in CoQ comes from the name Quinone. 

--- slide 37 ---
Electron Transport Chain
A cytochrome is a heme-containing protein in which reversible oxidation and reduction of an iron atom occur.
In cytochromes, the heme is bound to the protein in a way that prevents the heme from binding to O2 as it does when it is present in hemoglobin.

--- slide 38 ---
Electron Transport Chain: Complex 1
Complex I: NADH–coenzyme Q reductase
NADH from the citric acid cycle is the source of electrons processed in complex I.
The NAD+ generated can oxidize more substrates in oxidative pathways such as the citric acid cycle.
The hydrogen ions and electrons are transferred to the mobile electron carrier CoQ, forming CoQH2.

Oxidative Phosphorylation

--- slide 50 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
Oxidative phosphorylation is the synthesis of ATP from ADP:
ADP is phosphorylated.
Energy released during electron transport is used to synthesize ATP from ADP and Pi during oxidative phosphorylation.
The energy needed for phosphorylation comes from the oxidation of the coenzymes NADH and FADH2

--- slide 51 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
H+ ions do not directly participate in enzyme complex reactions (after the formation of CoQH2).
Instead, H+ ions become part of the cellular solution, where they participate in several reactions including the process by which ATP is synthesized.

--- slide 52 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
Three of the electron transport chain complexes also function as proton pumps – transfer protons from the matrix side of the inner mitochondrial membrane to the inter-membrane space.
Some of the H+ ions come from the reduced electron carriers, and some come from the matrix.

--- slide 53 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
Chemiosmotic model links the energy from electron transport to a hydrogen ion gradient.
The electron transport chain provides the energy to pump H+ ions across the inner membrane of the mitochondria.
The [H+] ions in the inter-membrane space becomes higher than that inside the matrix.
This creates a hydrogen ion gradient.

--- slide 54 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
To equalize the pH and charges, the H+ ions return to the matrix through a channel in the ATP synthase enzyme.

--- slide 55 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
The ATP synthase enzyme has two subunits:
the F0 subunit is the channel for proton flow.
the F1 subunit is the site for the formation of ATP.
As H+ return to the matrix through the F0 subunit energy is released and used for the synthesis of ATP in the F1 subunit.

--- slide 56 ---
Oxidative Phosphorylation
The ATP produced must be moved from the matrix back to the inter-membrane space before it can be used in metabolic reactions. 
For each ATP molecule transferred from the matrix to the inter-membrane space an ADP, a Pi, and an H+ ion move in the opposite direction.

--- slide 57 ---
Overall ATP Generation
ATP Yield from oxidative phosphorylation:
Each NADH entering the electron transport chain produces enough energy to make 2.5 ATPs.
Each FADH2 entering the electron transport chain produces enough energy to make 1.5 ATPs.

--- slide 58 ---
Regulation of Electron Transport and Oxidative Phosphorylation
Electron transport 
is regulated by the availability of ADP, Pi, oxygen (O2), and NADH.
decreases with low levels of any of these compounds and decreases the formation of ATP.
When a cell is active and ATP is consumed rapidly, the elevated levels of ADP will activate the synthesis of ATP.
The activity of electron transport is strongly dependent on the availability of ADP for ATP synthesis.

--- slide 59 ---
Complete Oxidation of Glucose
The complete oxidation of glucose to 6 CO2 and 6 H2O yields a maximum of 32 ATP.
C6H12O6 + 6O2    6CO2 + 6H2O (+ 32 ATP)

--- slide 61 ---
Chemistry Link to Health:Efficiency of ATP Production
Our cells are about 33% efficient in converting the total available chemical energy in glucose to ATP.
The remainder of the energy produced from glucose during the oxidation in our cells is lost as heat.

--- slide 63 ---
Conclusions
The citric acid cycle is a cyclic, 8-step, metabolic pathway
The citric acid cycle produces (in two rotations) 2 FADH2, 6 NADH, 4CO2 and 2 ATP.
The FADH2 and NADH are used in the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation to make 6H2O and 28 ATP.
Overall, each glucose molecule yields 6CO2, 6H2O, and 32 ATP.
