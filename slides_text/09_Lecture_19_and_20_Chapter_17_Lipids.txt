Lipids
Lipids are biomolecules that are soluble in non-polar solvents and insoluble in water.
Store chemical energy (triglycerides)
Materials in membranes (phospholipids)
Chemical messengers (steroids)
Lipids do not have one distinctive functional group. 
Carboxylic Acids, Esters, alcohols, ketones, alkenes, etc..
Lipids contain many non-polar C-C and C-H bonds and a few polar bonds
water insoluble

--- slide 25 ---
Trans-fats in Food
Saturated fat has no double bonds
Saturated with hydrogens
Unsaturated fats can be cis or trans
Trans configuration more stable
Fats added to processed food to provide desirable texture, taste, and firmness
Can by hydrogenated with H2 in presence of Ni or Pd catalyst
Controlling the degree of hydrogenation controls the melting point of the product
Diets high in saturated or trans fatty acids raise the ratio of LDL to HDL cholesterol and increase risk of cardiovascular disease.

--- slide 26 ---
Triacylglycerols – Health & Medicine
Trans fat has a similar 3D shape to saturated fats: Act like saturated fats in the body
A high intake of saturated triacylglycerols is linked to heart disease.
Saturated fats stimulate cholesterol synthesis, which can lead to cholesterol plaques building up inside arteries.
The result is high blood pressure, heart attack, and even stroke.

--- slide 27 ---
Triacylglycerols – Acid Hydrolysis
Triacylglycerols are hydrolyzed with water in the presence of an acid, base, or the lipase enzyme (in the body).
Hydrolysis: water splits molecule

--- slide 28 ---
Triacylglycerols – Hydrolysis
To metabolize triacylglycerols for energy, the esters are hydrolyzed by enzymes called lipases
Complete metabolism of a triacylglycerol yields CO2, H2O, and a great deal of energy.

--- slide 35 ---
Glycerophospholipids
Glycerophospholipids are the main component of most cell membranes.
Structurally, they resemble a triacylglycerol with a phosphodiester bonded to an alcohol replacing the third fatty acid.
[notes] Phosphodiester: two OR attached to P=O

--- slide 36 ---
Glycerophospholipids
Lecithin and cephalin are glycerophospholipids that
are abundant in brain and nerve tissues.
are found in egg yolk, wheat germ, and yeast.
Lecithin is used in mayonnaise to prevent the water and fat components to separate.
Fatty acid side chains form non-polar “tails”
Phosphodiester end of molecule is charged or polar “head”

--- slide 37 ---
Cell Membranes
The basic unit of living organisms is the cell
When phospholipids are mixed with water, they assemble into a lipid bilayer.
The polar heads orient on the outside to interact with polar water molecules.
The nonpolar tails orient on the inside to avoid polar water molecules.

--- slide 38 ---
Cell Membranes: Transportation and Diffusion
Substances are transported across a cell membrane by either diffusion, facilitated transport, or active transport.
Diffusion: Small non-polar molecules (O2 and CO2) and lipid soluble substances (steroid hormones)
higher to lower concentration (passive transport).
Facilitated transport: Larger polar molecules need to  use protein channels to increase the rate of diffusion.
Ions like Cl− or HCO3− travel through integral protein channels.
Active transport: Other ions, Na+, K+, and Ca2+, move against the concentration gradient; this requires energy input.

Phospholipids: Sphingophospholipids
Sphingophospholipids (or Sphingolipid) do not contain a glycerol backbone, they have a sphingosine backbone instead.
Sphingomyelins are a type of sphingolipid found around nerve cell axons in animals. They do not contain an ester; their single fatty acid is bonded to the backbone by an amide bond.
[notes] phosphocholine or phosphoethanolamine as their polar head group

Steroids
Steroids are a group of lipids whose carbon skeletons contain several fused rings.
Steroids do not contain fatty acids.
Non-hydrolyzable

--- slide 46 ---
Steroids: Cholesterol
Cholesterol is
synthesized in the liver.
obtained in the diet from many sources, including meat, cheese, butter, and eggs.
a component of cellular membranes, myelin sheaths, and brain and nerve tissues. 
the precursor of all steroid hormones and bile acids.
Elevated levels of cholesterol in the bloodstream lead to coronary artery disease, heart attack, etc.
Cholesterol has a hydroxyl group on carbon 3

--- slide 47 ---
Steroids: Bile Salts
Bile salts 
Synthesized in liver from the oxidation of cholesterol.
Stored in gallbladder and secreted into the small intestine.
Help the absorption and digestion of dietary fats (act like soaps to make fat soluble in water).
When large amounts of cholesterol accumulate in the gallbladder, gallstones are formed.

--- slide 48 ---

Steroid hormones are 
produced from cholesterol.
chemical messengers in cells.
Two important classes of steroid hormones include sex hormones and adrenal corticosteroids.
The female sex hormones are estrogens and progestins.
The male sex hormones are called androgens.

Fat Soluble Vitamins
Vitamins are organic compounds required in small quantities for normal metabolism and must be obtained from the diet.
Vitamins are either water soluble or fat soluble.
The four fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K) are found in fruits, vegetables, fish, liver, and dairy products.
They are stored in adipose cells to be used when needed.

Fat Soluble: Vitamin A
Vitamin A is found in liver, fish, and dairy products, and is made from β-carotene.
It is needed for vision and for healthy mucous membranes.
Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness and dry eyes and skin.

--- slide 57 ---
Fat Soluble: Vitamin A from Carrots

[notes] Isoprene: 2-Methylbuta-1,3-diene

--- slide 58 ---
Fat Soluble: Vitamin D
Vitamin D can be synthesized from cholesterol.
Multi-step process that starts with UVB radiation from the sun on your skin
It can be obtained in the diet from many foods, especially milk, and helps regulate Ca and K metabolism.
A deficiency of vitamin D causes rickets (bone malformation).

--- slide 59 ---
Fat-Soluble: Vitamin E
Vitamin E is an antioxidant, protecting unsaturated side chains in fatty acids from unwanted oxidation.
Deficiency of vitamin E causes numerous neurological problems, although it is rare.

--- slide 60 ---
Fat Soluble Vitamin K
Vitamin K regulates the synthesis of clotting proteins (prothrombin), and deficiency of this leads to excessive or fatal bleeding.

--- slide 61 ---
Eicosanoids
Eicosanoids are 
derived from the 20-carbon unsaturated fatty acids (eicosanoic acids).
are not stored in cells, but rather synthesized in response to external stimulus. 
Eicosanoids function as short-lived chemical messengers that act near their points of synthesis (“local hormones”). 
Prostaglandins, leukotrienes and thromboxanes are three types of eicosanoids.
[notes] (I – cos –an-oids)

--- slide 62 ---
Eicosanoids: Prostaglandins
Prostaglandins contain a five-membered ring
are formed from arachidonic acid, a polyunsaturated fatty acid with 20 carbon atoms.
differ by the substituents attached to the five-carbon ring. 
Prostaglandins are responsible for inflammation.
There are two different cyclooxygenase enzymes responsible for prostaglandin synthesis called COX-1 and COX-2.

--- slide 63 ---
NSAID Pain Relievers
Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like aspirin and ibuprofen relieve pain and inflammation by blocking the synthesis of these molecules.
They inactivate both COX-1 and -2,  but increase risk for stomach ulcer formation.
Long-term use of such products can result in liver, kidney, and gastrointestinal damage.

--- slide 64 ---
Eicosanoids: Leukotrienes
Leukotrienes have three double bonds (triene) that are conjugated (alternating double, single bonds)
Produced in Leukocytes (white blood cells). 
Responsible for inflammatory response. 
Leukotrienes are molecules that contribute to the asthmatic response by constricting smooth muscle of the lung.
New asthma drugs act by blocking the synthesis of leukotriene C4, which treats the disease instead of just the inflammation symptoms.
[notes] Produced in Leukocytes (White blood cells)
Role: Inflamation

--- slide 65 ---
Eicosanoids: Thromboxanes
Thromboxanes have a 6-membered ether containing ring
Thromboxanes promote platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction.
Aspirin and other NSAIDs inhibit the synthesis of thromboxanes by inhibiting the COX enzyme.

Conclusions
Lipids vary in functional groups but all contain numerous non-polar carbon-carbon and carbon-hydrogen bonds making them water insoluble
Lipids can be classified as:
Hydrolyzable: Fatty-acid based lipids. Contain ester group that can be hydrolyzed to form carboxylic acid and alcohol
Waxes
Triacylglycerols: Fats and Oils
Phospholipids: Cell Membranes
Non-hydrolyzable: No ester group
Steroids (Cholesterol, Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone)
Fat Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)
Eicosanoids (Prostaglandins, leukotrienes and thromboxanes)
