--- slide 9 ---
Isomers
Can carboxylic acids and esters exhibit skeletal isomerism?
Yes! With enough carbons, there can be skeletal isomers.
Can carboxylic acids exhibit positional isomerism?
No! Carboxylic acids are always terminal (Carbon 1).
Can esters exhibit positional isomerism?
Yes! Esters have two R groups, so the ester can shift between carbons on the chain.
Can carboxylic acids and esters be functional group isomers?
Yes! Carboxylic acids and esters with the same number of carbons can have the same molecular formula

Carboxylic Acids: Types of intermolecular forces
Can carboxylic acids form hydrogen bonds with other carboxylic acids?
Yes! They have an H bonded to an O
Can carboxylic acids have dipole-dipole intermolecular forces?
Yes! The carbonyl group C=O bond, C-O bond, and O-H bonds are polar, and the dipole moments do not cancel.
Can carboxylic acids have dispersion forces between molecules?
Yes, every molecule can have dispersion intermolecular forces

--- slide 28 ---
Esters: Types of intermolecular forces
Can esters form hydrogen bonds with other esters?
No! Like ethers, aldehydes, and ketones, there is no H bonded to an O, therefore they can not form hydrogen bonds to esters, ethers, aldehydes or ketones.
Can esters have dipole-dipole intermolecular forces?
Yes! The carbonyl group C=O bond, C-O bond, and O-R’ bonds are polar, and the dipole moments do not cancel.
Can esters have dispersion forces between molecules?
Yes, every molecule can have dispersion intermolecular forces

Solubility
Can carboxylic acids form hydrogen bonds with water?
Yes! They can both donate protons (hydrogens) to water and accept protons from water.
Can esters form hydrogen bonds with water?
Yes! They can accept protons from water, much like aldehydes and ketones.

--- slide 36 ---
Carboxylic Acids are Weak Acids
Carboxylic acids are proton (H+) donors:
They are weak acids compared to inorganic acids such as HCl or H2SO4.
Only a small percentage of a carboxylic acid is ionized in aqueous solution.

--- slide 37 ---
Neutralization of Carboxylic Acids
Carboxylic acids react with bases to form salts.
The acid-base reaction turns a water-insoluble carboxylic acid into a water-soluble carboxylic acid salt.

Preparation of Esters
The condensation reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol in the presence of a strong acid catalyst and heat is known as Fischer esterification.
Condensation reactions are two molecules combining with loss of small molecule (water, HCl, methanol, etc.)
Dehydration reactions are a subset of condensation reactions involving the loss of water
The –OR′ group replaces the –OH group.

Preparation of cyclic Esters
Hydroxy acids can undergo intramolecular esterification to form cyclic esters (lactones).

--- slide 45 ---
Ester to Carboxylic Acid
In a hydrolysis reaction an ester reacts with water to form a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.
Hydrolysis reaction is a chemical reaction where water is used to break a covalent bond
The –OH group replaces the –OR′ group.
Esters hydrolyze only very slowly, even in boiling water.
Hydrolysis becomes considerably more rapid, however, when the ester is heated in aqueous acid or base.

Carboxylic Acid to Ester and Back
Ester hydrolysis is the reverse reaction of esterification. These reactions require strong catalysts or enzymes.
