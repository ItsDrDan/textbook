DNA Replication
Each time a cell divides, an exact copy of the DNA of the parent cell is needed for the new daughter cell. 
DNA replication involves
unwinding the DNA
pairing the bases in each strand with new bases to form new complementary strands
producing two new DNA strands that exactly duplicate the original DNA

--- slide 31 ---
DNA Replication
During DNA Replication
an enzyme, helicase, unwinds the parent DNA at several sections
At each open DNA section (called a replication fork), DNA polymerase catalyzes the formation of 5′–3′ ester bonds of the leading strand
the lagging strand (growing in the 3′–5′ direction) is synthesized in short sections called Okazaki fragments 
DNA ligase joins the Okazaki fragments to give a single 3′–5′ DNA strand
Semi-conservative process: each new DNA double helix contains one strand of the parent.
leading strand, growing continuously
lagging strand, growing in small fragments
[notes] leading strand, growing continuously
lagging strand, growing in small fragments

--- slide 41 ---
Transcription: Synthesis of mRNA
In transcription, 
a section of DNA containing the gene unwinds
one strand of DNA is copied, starting at the initiation point (which has the sequence TATAAA)
an mRNA is synthesized using complementary base pairing, with uracil (U) replacing thymine(T)
the newly formed mRNA moves out of the nucleus to ribosomes in the cytoplasm
A gene is a segment of a DNA strand responsible for the production of a specific mRNA molecule.

--- slide 42 ---
Transcription: RNA Polymerase
The enzyme RNA polymerase forms the linkages between the ribonucleotides and the template DNA strand.
Transcription proceeds from the 3’ end to the 5’ end of the template.
Transcription ends when the RNA polymerase enzyme encounters a sequence of bases that is “read” as a stop signal.

--- slide 54 ---
Ribosomes
A ribosome is an rRNA–protein complex that serves as the site for the translation phase of protein synthesis.
4 rRNA molecules and 80 proteins in two subunits.
Each subunit: 65% rRNA and 35% protein.
The active site is located in the large ribosomal subunit. 
The active site is mostly rRNA.
The predominance of rRNA at the active site gives it the impression of an enzyme – ribozyme.
The mRNA binds to the small subunit of the ribosome.

--- slide 55 ---
Translation - Initiation
Initiation begins with mRNA binding to the P-site (peptidyl site) of the small ribosomal unit.
A tRNA brings the first amino acid, always at the start codon AUG (methionine).

--- slide 56 ---
Translation - Elongation
Elongation proceeds as the next tRNA molecule delivers the next amino acid.
The 2nd amino acid binds at the A site (aminoacyl site). 

--- slide 57 ---
Translation - Elongation
The enzyme peptidyl transferase links the P-site amino acid to the A-site amino acid to form a dipeptide. 
The 1st tRNA, which is now empty, leaves.

--- slide 58 ---
Translation - Elongation
The ribosome shifts on the mRNA by 3 base positions, i.e. a codon – translocation.
The new dipeptide is now at the P-site and a 3rd tRNA can bind to the A-site.

--- slide 59 ---
Translation - Elongation
A new peptide bond is formed.
The 2nd tRNA is released and mRNA moves by one codon. 
A 4th tRNA can bind to the newly empty P-site and so on – chain elongation.

--- slide 60 ---
Translation - Elongation

--- slide 61 ---
Translation - Termination
The polypeptide continues to grow via translocation until a stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA) is reached.
The polypeptide is then released from the last tRNA through hydrolysis.
The initial amino acid, methionine, is usually removed from the peptide chain.
Now have the amino acid sequence = 1o structure
 … then  2o, 3o, and 4o structures forms = protein is active

Genetic Mutations
A mutation is an error in base sequence in a gene that is reproduced during DNA replication. 
The error is passed on during transcription.
The error causes a change in the amino acid sequence during the protein synthesis.

--- slide 71 ---
Genetic Mutations
Some mutations are random, while others are caused by mutagens.
A mutagen is a substance or agent that causes a change in the structure of a gene.
Type of mutagens:
Radiation: UV light (skin cancer), X-rays, and radioactivity
Chemical agents: HNO2 (deamination of heterocyclic nitrogen bases, C becomes U)
Body has repair enzymes that recognize and replace altered bases.

--- slide 72 ---
Effects of Mutations
Some mutations do not cause significant changes in the primary structure of a protein.
For drastic changes in the amino acid sequence, the structure of the resulting protein may lose its biological activity.
Proteins no longer catalyze (i.e., enzymes), certain substances may accumulate in the cells until they are poisonous.
When a mutation causes a protein deficiency or defective protein synthesis and this mutation is passed through generations, it is a genetic disease.

--- slide 74 ---
Viruses
Viruses
are small particles of DNA or RNA that require a host cell to replicate
cause a viral infection when the DNA or RNA enters a host cell
are synthesized in the host cell from the viral RNA produced by viral DNA

--- slide 75 ---
Reverse Transcription
In reverse transcription,
a retrovirus, which contains viral RNA but no viral DNA, enters a cell
the viral RNA uses reverse transcriptase to produce a viral DNA strand
the viral DNA strand forms a complementary DNA strand
the new DNA uses the nucleotides and enzymes in the host cell to synthesize new virus particles

Conclusions
Nucleosides consist of one of five bases bonded to an aldopentose
Purine Bases: Adenine, Guanine
