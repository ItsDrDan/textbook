Chapter 12 
Classify molecules as being saturated or unsaturated hydrocarbons. 
Understand the basic differences between organic and inorganic molecules
Classify molecules as cyclic, acyclic, alkane, alkene, alkyne or aromatic. 
Identify properties that are characteristic of hydrocarbons (solubility, melting point, boiling point, flammability) 
Identify molecular geometry around a carbon (tetrahedral, trigonal planar, linear, etc) 
Draw expanded, condensed, and line-angle structural formulas (with alkyl branches and halogen substituents) for: 
Alkanes 
Cycloalkanes
Alkenes (cis and trans isomers) 
Cycloalkenes
Alkynes 
Interconvert IUPAC names and structures for alkanes, cycloalkanes, alkenes, cycloalkenes, alkynes and aromatic compounds (with alkyl branches and halogen substituents). 
Balance combustion reactions for hydrocarbons 
Predict the product of addition (& elimination) reactions to alkenes and alkynes following 
Markovnikov’s rule (or Zeitsev’s Rule for elimination) 
Hydrogenation (dehydrogenation)
Hydrohalogenation (dehydrohalogenation) 
Halogenation (dehalogenation) 
Hydration (dehydration) 
Describe the bonding in benzene and what differentiates an aromatic compound from an alkene 
Classify carbons as being primary, secondary, tertiary, or quaternary carbons 
Identify molecules as constitutional isomers (skeletal, positional),stereoisomers (cis or trans), or the same molecule. 
Be able to name and draw products of basic reactions discussed in lab or lecture.
Chapter 13 
Identify alcohols, phenols, ethers and thiols in organic molecules (i.e. be able to circle and label these functional groups) 
Interconvert between IUPAC name and structure for alcohols, phenols, ethers and thiols 
Molecules will only have one suffix-worthy functional group
Molecules may have alkyl or halogen substituents 
Classify alcohols as being primary, secondary or tertiary alcohols 
Describe the types of intermolecular forces possible for hydrocarbons, alcohols, ethers, thiols and phenols. 
Identify the intermolecular forces present between two molecules.
Understand the role of polarity and electronegativity in IMF
Understand how it has an effect on solubility
Predict relative solubility, melting point, and boiling point for a set of molecules (hydrocarbons, 
alcohols, ethers, etc.) and explain reasoning in terms of intermolecular forces. 
Predict products for the combustion, dehydration and oxidation of alcohols 
Describe key features of each reaction classification
Identify relationships between reaction classifications (i.e. oxidation/reduction and addition/elimination) 
Draw products of the oxidation of thiols (disulfide bonds) 
Identify molecules as constitutional isomers (skeletal, positional, or functional group), stereoisomers (cis or trans), or the same molecule.
