What are Amino Acids?
Amino Acids are the building blocks of proteins
Amino Acids contain two functional groups:
A carboxylic acid group (COOH)
An amine group (NH2) attached to the α-carbon
Classified by “R” group of side chain

--- slide 8 ---
Classification of R Groups
The R group, called the side chain, determines the identity of the amino acid.
Hydrophilic 
Basic: Contains N (or N+) on side chain with available lone pairs
Acidic: Contains COOH (COO-) on side chain
Neutral: Capable of H-bonding, or polar
Hydrophobic (Non-polar)

Zwitterions
Since amino acids contain a base (NH2) and an acid (COOH), a proton-transfer occurs from the acid to the base forming a zwitterion.
Zwitterion: Overall neutral charge, but has charged areas of molecule

--- slide 14 ---
Isoelectric Point (pI)
The isoelectric point is the pH at which the compound has an overall neutral charge
Most amino acids have a pI around 5.0-6.0
Amino acids with an acidic R have a pI < 4.0
Amino acids with a basic R have a pI > 6.0

Electrophoresis
In electrophoresis, an electric current is used to separate amino acids based on their pI.
Buffer solutions of various pH can be used to control the charge on the amino acids
the positively charged amino acids move toward the negative electrode. 
the negatively charged amino acids move toward the positive electrode. 
an amino acid at its pI does not migrate. 
the amino acids are identified as separate bands on the filter paper or thin layer plate.

All amino acids (but glycine) have a chirality center on the a carbon.

Peptide Bonds
Peptides and proteins are formed when amino acids are joined together by amide bonds.
A dipeptide has two amino acids joined together by one amide bond.
Oligopeptides have 2-20 amino acids, 
Polypeptides have 21-40 amino acids, 
Proteins have more than 40 amino acids.
Condensation: Molecules join, small molecule (H2O) lost
[notes] Condensation reaction

Neuropeptides
Enkephalins belong to the family of polypeptides called endorphins, which are known for their pain reducing and mood enhancing effects.
Enkephalins, pentapeptides made in the brain, act as pain killers and sedatives by binding to pain receptors.
Addictive drugs morphine and heroin bind to these same pain receptors, thus producing a similar physiological response, though longer lasting.
[notes] En-Kef-alin

--- slide 34 ---
Example
Oxytocin and vasopressin are cyclic nonapeptide hormones, which have identical sequences except for two amino acids
The slightly different sequence gives the two peptides vastly different effects on the body.
Oxytocin stimulates the contraction of uterine muscles, and signals for milk production; it is  often used to induce labor.
Vasopressin, antidiuretic hormone (ADH) targets the kidneys and helps to limit urine production to keep body fluids up during dehydration.

Protein Structure
Protein function depends on structure
Protein structure can be described on many levels:
Primary Structure: The amino acid sequence
Secondary Structure: The “local” hydrogen-bonding scheme
Tertiary Structure: How the polypeptide, with all of its regions of secondary structure (a-helix, b-sheet) has folded over upon itself.  
Interaction between R-groups is important
Quaternary Structure: How protein subunits aggregate into a larger functional unit.

--- slide 42 ---
Proteins: Secondary Structure
The secondary structure is due to hydrogen bonding between amino acids in the backbone of protein chain.
Intramolecular hydrogen bonding: same molecule
N-H of one amino acid, and C=O of another
Two stable arrangements are the a-helix and the b-pleated sheet.
Which one forms depends on which one maximizes hydrogen bonding (some R groups are bulky)
Most proteins have regions of a-helix and b-pleated sheet, and other regions that are random arrangements.

--- slide 43 ---
The a-Helix
The coiled shape of the a-helix is due to the hydrogen bonds between the H of an N-H group of an amino acid and the O of C=O of the fourth amino acid down the chain.
The R groups stick out of the helix

--- slide 44 ---
β-Sheet
 A b-pleated sheet consists of polypeptide chains arranged side by side with hydrogen bonds holding the sheets together.
Ribbons are anti-parallel. 
[notes] Notice how ribbons alternate direction: chains are anti-parallel. One is C-terminus at the top, the other is C-terminus at the top.

--- slide 52 ---
Quaternary Structure
Hemoglobin:
has 4 polypeptide chains, each carrying a heme unit.
Fe2+ coordinates to O2 to transport it through the body
has two a chains with 141 amino acids and two b chains with 146 amino acids.
Carbon monoxide (CO) is poisonous because it binds 200 times more strongly to the Fe2+ than does O2.

--- slide 58 ---
Protein Hydrolysis
Protein hydrolysis:
involves breaking the peptide bonds by treatment with aqueous acid, base, or certain enzymes to give smaller peptides and amino acids. 
occurs in the digestion of proteins.
In the body, the enzyme pepsin in gastric juice cleaves some of the peptide bonds of large proteins to make smaller peptide chains.
In the intestines, enzymes trypsin and chymotrypsin hydrolyze the remainder of the amide bonds resulting in individual amino acids.

--- slide 59 ---
Protein Hydrolysis
In the lab, the hydrolysis of a peptide requires acid or base, water, and heat.

--- slide 60 ---
Protein Denaturation
Denaturation:
is the process of altering the shape of a protein without breaking the amide bonds that form the primary structure.
renders the protein biologically inactive.
often makes globular proteins less water soluble.
Interfere with intramolecular forces

Almost all amino acids have a chiral alpha carbon

Secondary

Quaternary
