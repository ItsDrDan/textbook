Classification of Alcohols
Alcohols are classified by the number of C atoms bonded to the C with the OH group
A primary (1o) alcohol has an –OH group on a C bonded only to 1 other C.
A secondary (2o) alcohol has an –OH group on a C bonded only to 2 other C.
A tertiary (3o) alcohol has an –OH group on a C bonded only to 3 other C.

--- slide 29 ---
Intermolecular Forces
Alcohols & Phenols
The oxygen has a bent geometry and two polar bonds: Net dipole (polar!)
Hydrogen-bonding between molecules
H bonded to O is attracted to lone pairs on another oxygen
Ethers & Thiols
The oxygen/sulfur has bent geometry and two polar bonds: Net dipole (polar)
No hydrogen-bonding (requires an H bonded to an O, N or F) between other ether and thiol molecules
Ethers can hydrogen bond to R-OH!

--- slide 30 ---
Solubility
Alcohols, Phenols & Ethers
Can form hydrogen bonds with water
Alcohols and phenol are acceptors and donors
Ethers are only acceptors. 
Ethers are less soluble than alcohols with similar alkyl group lengths.
Degree of solubility in water depends on length of alkyl chain
Generally, 4 carbons or less = soluble
Dimethyl ether in water

Physical Properties: Boiling and Melting Points
Boiling point increases with size of alkyl chain (more dispersion forces)
For molecules of similar molecular weight (similar size alkyl groups):
Look at what intermolecular forces are possible
Look at shape of molecule (straight or branched)
Stronger forces = higher melting and boiling points
Straight-chain alcohols with <12 C are liquids at room temperature

--- slide 46 ---
Metabolism of Methanol
Ingestion of methanol can cause blindness (~15 mL) and death (100-250 mL)
Methanol is oxidized by the same enzymes as ethanol
Methanol is extremely toxic because of the products produced by this oxidation

--- slide 47 ---
Reactions of Thiols
Oxidation & Reduction
Thiols can be oxidized  to disulfides
Disulfides can be reduced into thiols
When hair is “permed”, disulfide bonds in the hair are reduced, then oxidized once the hair is rolled around curlers

--- slide 48 ---
Conclusions
Hydrogen-bonding is the strongest intermolecular force
Hydrogen bonding occurs between an H (bonded to O, N, F), and an O,N,F on another molecule
Alcohols can hydrogen bond to other alcohols or water
Ethers can not hydrogen bond to ethers, but can accept H’s from water to form hydrogen bonds
If intermolecular forces are similar to water, it’ll be soluble in water
The stronger the intermolecular forces, the higher the boiling and melting points
Alcohols can be dehydrated and oxidized
Dehydration is an example of an elimination reaction (loose water)
Oxidation can occur with primary and secondary alcohols (turned into aldehydes or ketones).

--- slide 49 ---
Conclusions
Alcohols, ethers, thiols, and phenols have oxygens/sulfur attached to carbon with a single-bond
If there is a C=O, it’s another functional group!

-ol = alcohol suffix
-thiol = thiol suffix
-phenol = phenol parent
oxy- = ether prefix
