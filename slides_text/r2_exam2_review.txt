CHEM 3B – Exam 2 – Chapters 14,16, 18
Key Skills 
This document provides a list of key skills from each chapter that students are expected to be able to perform for the exam. Skills may be tested individually, combined with other skills, or not at all. Although the exam will primarily focus on chapters since the previous exam, students are expected to know concepts from the previous exam. For example, isomerism, oxidation of alcohols, and nomenclature are concepts that are expanded on in current chapters.

Chapter 14 – Ketones and Aldehydes 
Identify molecules as containing ketone or aldehyde functional groups 
Name molecules containing ketone and/or aldehyde functional groups following IUPAC nomenclature rules 
Molecules may contain substituents off of carbon chain such as alcohols, alkyl groups, or halogens 
Predict relative solubility in water for several molecules containing aldehyde and ketone functional groups 
Predict relative boiling points and melting points for several molecules containing aldehyde or ketone functional groups. 
Describe the types of intermolecular forces between two aldehyde or ketone molecules, or with water and explain how these intermolecular forces influence physical properties. 
Predict the products from the reaction of aldehydes and ketones with Tollen’s reagent, and Benedict’s reagent. 
If no reaction, write no reaction 
Predict the products of the oxidation and reduction of aldehydes and ketones
If no reaction, write no reaction 
Identify molecules as being hemiacetals or acetals 
Draw the products of the reaction between an alcohol and a ketone or aldehyde. 
Identify chiral carbons in a molecule 
Identify molecules as being enantiomers, diastereomers, or identical molecules. 
From a Fischer projection, identify stereoisomers as being either D- or L-. 
Chapter 16 – Carboxylic Acids and Esters 
Identify molecules as containing carboxylic acid or ester functional groups. 
Name molecules containing carboxylic acid or ester functional groups using IUPAC nomenclature rules 
Molecules may contain other functional groups as substituents including alkyl groups, alcohols, halogens, and aldehydes.
Predict relative solubility in water for several molecules containing carboxylic acid or ester functional groups
Molecules containing other functional groups such as alcohols, ethers, aldehydes and ketones may be present.
Predict relative boiling points and melting points for several molecules carboxylic acid or ester functional groups
Molecules containing other functional groups such as alcohols, ethers, aldehydes and ketones may be present. 
Describe the types of intermolecular forces between two carboxylic acid or ester molecules, or with water, and explain how these intermolecular forces influence physical properties. 
Describe the acid/base behavior of a carboxylic acid in water. 
Predict the products of the reduction of carboxylic acids 
Recognize connection to aldehyde, and alcohol oxidation
Write the products of the reaction between a strong base (such as KOH, NaOH) with a carboxylic acid (acid/base neutralization)
Describe relative solubility of carboxylate salts to carboxylic acids 
Write the structure of the ester produced from an alcohol reacting with a carboxylic acid (condensation/esterification). 
Write the carboxylic acid produced from the acid or base hydrolysis of an ester. 
Base hydrolysis (saponification) is hydrolysis, followed by acid/base reaction  
Chapter 18 – Amines and Amides 
Identify molecules as containing amine and amide functional groups o Including heterocyclic molecules 
Classify amines as primary, secondary, tertiary, or quaternary. 
Name amines and amides following IUPAC nomenclature rules 
Molecules may contain functional groups from previous chapters such as carboxylic acids, esters, aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, alkanes, and halogens. 
Predict relative boiling points and melting points of amine and amide molecules
Molecules containing other functional groups from previous chapters may also be present. 
Describe the types of intermolecular forces between two amine or amide molecules, or with water, and explain how these intermolecular forces influence physical properties. 
Describe the acid/base properties of an amine in water 
Draw the products of an acid/base neutralization reaction (amines with carboxylic acids, amines with strong acids) 
Explain why many pharmaceuticals are sold as ammonium salts rather than amines. 
Draw the amide formed from the amidation (condensation) of a carboxylic acid with an amine 
Note: Only primary and secondary amines can undergo amidation.
Draw the carboxylic acid formed from the acid or base hydrolysis of an amide
Describe what a neurotransmitter is, and how neurotransmitters are stored, released, and received.
