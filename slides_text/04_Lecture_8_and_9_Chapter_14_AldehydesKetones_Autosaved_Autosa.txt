Nomenclature: Compounds with multiple functional groups
If a compound has more than one functional group, the functional group in the suffix is highest priority
Generally, more oxidized functional groups take priority (more C-O bonds)
Lower priority functional groups become part of the prefix with substituent names

Stereoisomers: Chirality
Stereoisomers always have the same connectivity of atoms, but different arrangement in space
A stereocenter is a carbon that is bonded to four different groups
Chiral objects have non-superimposable mirror images

--- slide 68 ---
Conclusions
Aldehydes and Ketones are two carbonyl functional groups (C=O)
The carbonyl functional group is polar
Dipole-dipole intermolecular forces
Aldehydes and ketones are proton-acceptors to form hydrogen bonds with water, but lack any H’s to form hydrogen bonds to other aldehydes and ketones
Higher melting point and boiling points than alkanes, but lower than alcohols
Aldehydes can be oxidized, ketones can’t.
Aldehydes and ketones can be reduced to alcohols
Alcohols can add to the carbonyl group making hemiacetals, hemiketals, acetals and ketals
Stereoisomers have the same connectivity of atoms, but in different orientations in space.
A chiral molecule must have at least one chiral center (stereocenter)
