Chemistry 3B – Exam 3 – Chapters 15, 17, 19, 20
Key Skills
This document provides a list of key skills from each chapter that students are expected to be
able to perform for the exam. Skills may be tested individually, combined with other skills, or
not at all. Although the exam will primarily focus on chapters since the previous exam, students
are expected to know concepts from earlier in the semester such as identifying functional
groups, chiral carbons and isomers, and common reactions of each functional group (oxidation,
reduction, addition, hydrolysis, etc)

    •   The reference sheet provided will have Fischer projections of monosaccharides, amino
        acids names. The D- and L- notation will not be specified.
    •   The reference sheet will also provide line-structures of common fatty acids with names.
    •   Remember the reference sheet will not include how to draw Haworth Projections

Chapter 15
    •   Classify a carbohydrate as an aldose, ketose, and indicate the number of carbon atoms
    •   Identify and draw the D- and L- configurations (Fischer Projections) for
        monosaccharides
    •   Classify a carbohydrate as a monosaccharide, disaccharide, or polysaccharide
    •   Know which monosaccharides make-up the disaccharides and polysaccharides we
        discussed and how they are joined together (glycosidic bond numbering)
    •   Describe the relationship between two carbohydrate molecules (enantiomers, epimers,
        etc).
    •   Convert from Fischer projection to Haworth projection.
    •   Identify which carbon is the anomeric carbon
    •   Draw the α and β anomers
    •   Draw the product of oxidation/reduction reactions of monosaccharides
    •   Identify carbohydrates as reducing sugars – and know what structural feature makes it a
        reducing sugar.
    •   Identify the numbering of glycosidic linkages between monosaccharide units given drawing of
        disaccharide and determine if it is α or β linkage.
    •   Identify a reaction as a condensation reaction, or hydrolysis reaction.
    •   Be able to tell which where bonds break, and new bonds form, and identify functional groups
        involved.
    •   Describe the structural similarities and differences between amylose, amylopectin, cellulose,
        and glycogen

Chapter 17
   •   Classify lipids based on molecular structure as being: fatty acid, wax, triacylglycerols,
       glycerphospholipids, sphingolipids, steroids, fat-soluble vitamins or eicosanoids.
   •   Identify lipids, based on structure, as being hydrolyzable or non-hydrolyzable.
   •   Identify lipids, based on structure, as being saturated, monounsaturated or polyunsaturated.
   •   If unsaturated, identify the isomer as being cis or trans
   •   If unsaturated, identify location of double bond based on omega nomenclature
   •   Predict which fatty acid, wax, or triacylglycerol would have the lowest/highest boiling point or
       melting point based on structure.
   •   Match lipid classification to descriptions of biological function
   •   Draw the products of the formation of (condensation), or hydrolysis of, a hydrolyzable lipid
       (wax, triacylglycerol, etc.)


Chapter 19
   •   Given structures of amino acids, classify the amino acid R group as being acidic, basic, polar,
       non- polar, hydrophilic and/or hydrophobic
   •   Using the Zwitterion form of an amino acid as a reference point, draw the structure at pH’s
       above/below the isoelectric point.
   •   Draw the structure of a peptide and give the three-letter or one-letter abbreviations for the
       peptide
   •   Match the name of a peptide to the structure
   •   Describe the distinctions between a protein’s primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary
       structure
   •   Draw the structure(s) of the products/reactants for the condensation of two amino acids or
       hydrolysis of a dipeptide.
   •   Distinguish between protein hydrolysis and protein denaturization.
   •   Describe various ways to denature a protein.


Chapter 20
   •   Describe enzymes and their role in biological reactions
   •   Describe models of enzyme action (induced fit, lock & key).
   •   Classify enzymes by the type of reaction they catalyze (given a chemical equation of an enzyme
       catalyzed reaction, or description of it.)
   •   Describe the effect of changes of temperature, pH, and concentration on enzyme activity
   •   Describe the role of allosteric enzymes, feedback control, and covalent modification in
       regulating enzyme activity.
   •   Describe competitive/non-competitive inhibition and reversible/irreversible inhibition
   •   Describe the types of cofactors found in enzymes.
